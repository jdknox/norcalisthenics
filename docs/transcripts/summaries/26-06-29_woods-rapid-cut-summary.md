# How I dropped to sub-10% body fat in 17 days
2026-06-29

[In this video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bKOcsk3ZJHI) Woods describes how he "dropped a rapid amount of body fat in just over two weeks" without anything extreme.

Below is a paraphrased summary, not a transcript.

## Core argument
- Standard advice (≈500 kcal/day deficit → ~1 lb/week loss, ~3,500 kcal per lb of fat) is theoretically correct but hard to execute consistently. Most people oscillate around maintenance and net out with little progress.
- His proposed fix: deliberately overshoot the deficit for a short window (2–4 weeks) to generate visible momentum and build confidence that the approach works, then relax back toward a sustainable level.
- This aggressive approach is framed as unsuitable for long cuts (12+ weeks, due to muscle-loss risk) but fine short-term.

## Numbers / protocol
- **Maintenance calorie estimate:** bodyweight (kg) × 33.
- **Protein target:** 1.8 g per kg of goal bodyweight per day (protein is emphasized as the key lever for preserving muscle during a deficit).
- **Deficit target:** roughly double the standard 500 kcal/day, i.e. ~1,000 kcal/day below maintenance.
- **Duration:** 2–4 weeks for this aggressive phase.

## Example day (his own numbers)
- ~500 g meat → ~700 kcal, ~100 g protein.
- 6 eggs cooked in butter → ~500 kcal, ~40 g protein.
- Optional cheese for a bit more protein.
- Subtotal: ~140–150 g protein, ~1,400 kcal — protein target already met.
- Add 2–3 servings of fruit/carbs (~200–300 kcal) for training energy and appetite management.
- Total: ~1,600–1,700 kcal/day.
- His own stats: bodyweight ~80 kg, protein target 140 g/day, maintenance estimate ~2,600 kcal, resulting deficit ~1,000 kcal/day.

## Reported outcome
- ~80 kg → ~77.5 kg over 2.5 weeks (self-estimated, no scale access — "fairly good idea of my body").
- ≈1 kg/week loss, consistent with the ~1,000 kcal/day deficit math.

## Execution notes
- Recommends designing the day "bottom-up": protein sources first, then a small amount of carbs, then stop — deliberately leaving a large buffer between calories eaten and maintenance.
- Advocates cooking your own food to reduce estimation error during this phase.
- Track bodyweight daily and average it (he didn't, due to travel, and flags this as a deviation from his own recommended method).
- Explicitly says to eat more on days of high hunger (adds fruit/chocolate) rather than forcing strict adherence — treats hunger/appetite as the primary feedback signal for how far to push, with strict targets used only as a starting map.

## Not covered in the source

- No macro breakdown of fats/carbs beyond the general categories above.
