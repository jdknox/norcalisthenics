# The diet that works when you stop tracking every meal
2026-06-01

[In this video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=h3T5IH4AcS0) Woods opens with a blunt claim: "Most diets are designed to fail you."

Below is a paraphrased summary, not a transcript.

## Core argument
- What determines body composition is the weekly average of intake, not any individual meal — a string of "perfect" days can be undone by one high-calorie day, and vice versa.
- Most diet plans fail because they require constant, full-time adherence (every meal, every day) with no margin for error — a single deviation feels like failure and often triggers abandoning the diet entirely.

## The "barbell" approach
- Split eating into two extremes rather than moderating every meal: one (or two) deliberately high-quality, target-hitting meal(s) per day, and complete freedom for all other food.
- The prepared/intentional meal(s) prioritize, in order: protein target, then nutrient-dense whole foods (vegetables, healthy fats, flavor), leaving carbohydrates largely to come from "free" eating elsewhere in the day.
- Because the deliberate meal already meets the daily protein and satiety needs, the remaining calorie budget can be spent on anything (dining out, alcohol, treats) without breaking the target average.

## Concrete numbers (his own example)
- Protein source example: ~500 g (1 lb) of meat/day ≈ 100 g protein; plus incidental protein from other foods (yogurt, eggs, dinner out) to close the gap to his ~140 g/day target.
- Stated calorie cost of his full protein/nutrient-dense meal + morning fruit: roughly 1,200 kcal at maintenance, or as low as ~900 kcal when in a cutting phase — leaving roughly half the day's energy budget free for anything else.
- Food categories for the "intentional" side: primary protein (regional/seasonal meat or seafood), eggs, dairy (or high/no-fat Greek yogurt for vegetarians), vegetables/aromatics for flavor, fermented foods, and fruit/honey specifically for carbs (prioritized pre-workout).

## Practical takeaway
- The deliberate meal(s) can be as simple as seared ground meat, or built up with vegetables/spices/condiments to taste; carbs are treated as easy to get incidentally and aren't specifically planned for outside pre-workout timing.
- Framed as two categories only: nail one genuinely high-quality, target-hitting meal, then eat freely the rest of the day — repeated daily, this produces the desired weekly average without requiring meal-by-meal tracking.
