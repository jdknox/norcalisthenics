# How to consistently lose body fat (while eating more)
2024-11-19

[In this video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ycsIITbDzrQ) Woods names the failure mode directly: "The worst possible position you can be in when trying to lose body fat" is dieting hard and still not losing it.

Below is a paraphrased summary, not a transcript. Core content (build muscle before/during cutting, moderate deficit, 12-week cycles) overlaps heavily with several other summarized nutrition videos — this one's distinct framing is the "micro vs. macro" split for avoiding an overly aggressive deficit.

## Sequencing argument (consistent with his other content)
- States that unless someone is significantly overweight, the first priority before any intentional weight loss should be establishing strength training + a protein target — muscle mass is what prevents "losing weight" from becoming "just getting skinny," and what allows the eventual leaner result to be sustained rather than rebounding.
- Notes that simply starting strength training and hitting a protein target often produces some fat loss by default, before any deliberate calorie restriction is added.

## The "micro vs. macro" framing (specific to this video)
- **Micro level (per-day intensity):** the main mistake is making the daily deficit too large — going beyond roughly 20% (about 500 kcal/day) is described as reliably triggering hormonal/energy/mood disruption and hunger that eventually causes bingeing or abandoning the diet. Target instead: a pace of about 0.5 kg (1 lb)/week.
- **Macro level (how long a deficit period runs):** even at a moderate daily pace, running a deficit for too many consecutive weeks causes cumulative fatigue and hormonal downregulation — capped at 12-week blocks, followed by a 2-week break at maintenance to reset before another block if needed.
- Frames these as two separate dials that both need to be kept moderate — getting the daily rate right but running it too long (or vice versa) both lead back to the same burnout/rebound problem.

## Reiterated end state
- Once a desired leanness level is reached, the focus shifts back to muscle-building; continued muscle gain over time is described as making leanness easier to maintain without further aggressive dieting, consistent with the "healthy weight range" model detailed in his other nutrition content.
