# How to actually get lean, step by step (40min/week)
2025-02-04

[In this video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cnGmf6M18fk) Woods promises: "I'm going to show you exactly how you can get lean and stay there forever."

Below is a paraphrased summary, not a transcript. The core "healthy weight range" and "build muscle before cutting" model overlaps substantially with his other summarized nutrition content (e.g. "why trying to stay lean is killing your muscle gains," "how to maintain a six-pack without hard work") — this summary focuses on the specific numbers and framing unique to this video.

## Core model
- Same healthy-weight-range concept as his other content: getting leaner ultimately requires replacing body fat with muscle mass, not just losing weight — because losing weight without building muscle drops a person below their body's comfortable weight range, triggering hunger/appetite pushback and making the loss unsustainable.
- Worked numerical example: someone at 85 kg / 25% body fat would need to drop to 71 kg to reach 10% body fat with *no* added muscle (a large, likely unsustainable cut) — but adding just 5 kg of muscle first would allow reaching the same 10% body fat at ~77 kg, a much smaller and more sustainable weight change. Used to illustrate how much easier "getting lean" becomes once adequate muscle is built first.

## Two-factor framework (consistent with his other content)
- **Training** sends the signal for muscle growth; **protein intake** (1.8 g/kg goal bodyweight/day) supplies the material for it — once both are in place, muscle growth rate becomes limited by the body's own biological pace, not by additional effort.
- Once muscle-building is underway, a moderate calorie deficit (~500 kcal/day, targeting ~0.5 kg/week loss) is used to remove the remaining body fat layer — explicitly capped at roughly 12 weeks per cycle, with a break (return to maintenance) if more fat loss is still wanted afterward.

## Personal account specific to this video
- Describes an early-career mistake: cutting down to ~10% body fat before building adequate muscle, which left him feeling "scrawny" rather than looking the way he wanted — required a subsequent period of deliberately regaining weight (including some fat) to build a proper muscle base before cutting again more successfully.
- Separately, describes his legs specifically as a long-neglected weak point — crediting several months of consistent (once-weekly) leg training as what finally allowed him to reach a shredded look, even without significantly tightening his diet at the time — framed as evidence that muscle development across the *whole* body (not just visible/priority areas) is what ultimately determines how lean someone can look at a given weight.

## Answers to common questions (addressed explicitly in this video)
- **Cardio:** not required for fat loss in this model — energy expenditure from cardio is automatically accounted for by tracking body-weight trend against the calorie plan; recommends doing cardio only for enjoyment/wellbeing, not as a deliberate deficit lever.
- **Diet type/macros/fasting/meal timing:** framed as personal preference with no specific requirement, as long as the daily protein target and overall calorie target are met — recommends whatever eating pattern is most enjoyable and sustainable, generally favoring less-processed whole foods for the majority of intake.
