# The one simple daily meal that transformed my physique
2025-06-16

[In this video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zy497DOahJ8) Woods poses it as a thought experiment: "What if I told you there was one meal you could eat every day" that solved most of nutrition?

Below is a paraphrased summary, not a transcript. This is the fullest single-video treatment of his "one big protein meal" habit (briefly referenced in several other summarized videos).

## Why protein specifically
- Argues protein is the one nutrition variable that doesn't happen "by default" and has outsized consequences if missed: insufficient protein both blunts the body's ability to respond to training (slower muscle gain, slower recomposition) and — because appetite is the body's only mechanism for signaling a protein shortfall — drives general hunger and overeating regardless of total calorie intake.
- Frames correct protein intake as the necessary precondition before calorie intake is even worth managing — sequences priorities as: training first, then protein, then (only if needed) calorie deficit — and advises against trying to fix multiple habits simultaneously.

## Target and framing
- Stated target: 1.8 g protein per kg of goal bodyweight/day (his own example: 78 kg → 140 g/day) — describes this as a one-time calculation, not something to recalculate regularly.
- General range cited for most people: roughly 110–160 g/day.

## Three stated reasons people fail to sustain adequate protein intake
1. **Unpalatable food choices** (bland meals, protein shakes) that aren't enjoyable long-term.
2. **Spreading protein across many small meals** (3–5 meals/day) — argued to be unnecessary friction, not a physiological requirement; explicitly states the "max ~30g protein absorbed per sitting" claim is a myth not supported by research, and that a much larger single-sitting protein intake is used effectively by the body.
3. **Excessive meal-prep effort** (individually portioned meals) that consumes unnecessary time.

## His protocol
- Get the bulk of the daily protein target (100+ g) in one meal, ideally eaten alone/for oneself anyway (his choice: lunch) — since timing doesn't matter, this can be placed wherever is most convenient and least disruptive to social eating later in the day.
- Simple food math used: roughly 500 g (about 1 lb) of any meat (chicken, fish, beef, pork, lamb) ≈ 100 g protein and ~700 kcal; paired with cheese, eggs, or other protein sources as desired to close the remaining gap to target.
- Weekly batch-cooking method: a slow cooker loaded with ~3 kg of meat once a week (~8 hours on high), portioned into containers for Monday–Saturday, with a freshly cooked meal (steak, burger patties) on the remaining day — total weekly prep/cook/clean time stated at under 30 minutes.
- Flavor/enjoyment treated as essential to long-term adherence, not incidental: uses cheese, sauces, spices, and varies ingredients over time specifically to avoid boredom with the daily meal.

## Rest of the day
- Once the protein target is essentially locked in by lunch, the remaining daily calories (his example: ~1,500–2,000 kcal depending on maintenance vs. deficit) are unrestricted — any other meal, snack, or drink is treated as free, since the protein/appetite mechanism described above is expected to naturally regulate total intake once it's satisfied.
