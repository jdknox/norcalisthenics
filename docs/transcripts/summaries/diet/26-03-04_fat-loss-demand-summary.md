# How to lose body fat on demand
2026-03-04

[In this video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y_cHv_wYTUI) Woods frames the appeal: if "you could just drop body fat on demand," you'd never need another fitness fad.

Below is a paraphrased summary, not a transcript.

## Three-stage model — what stops people from reliably losing fat
### Mistake 1 — chasing indirect causes instead of the direct one
- The only actual mechanism for losing body fat: a sustained calorie deficit. Everything else (specific foods, exercise types, supplements) is indirect at best.
- Warns against a pattern of trying various indirect methods, each failure eroding confidence, rather than starting from the one mechanism that actually works.
- Position: cardio specifics and detailed nutrition tweaks may make the process more pleasant/sustainable, but aren't the actual lever — and should be part of a routine regardless of whether currently trying to lose fat.

### Mistake 2 — poor execution of the deficit itself
- Common failure mode: meticulous tracking that still doesn't produce weight loss — attributed to a mismatch between tracked and actual intake, not metabolism or genetics.
- Fix: track results (daily body weight, weekly averages) rather than obsessing over tracking inputs perfectly — use the objective output (is weight actually trending down) to adjust intake, rather than assuming a "stalled metabolism."
- Recommends keeping the food plan itself simple (a short, consistent list of what to eat and roughly how much) so it's easy to identify and adjust the actual source of error if results stall.

### Mistake 3 — making the deficit harder than necessary
- **Sub-mistake A — "eating clean":** conflates fat loss with giving up all enjoyable food. Argues you only need to be in deficit for as long as it takes to reach your goal (which could be weeks to several months depending on starting body fat) — restricting food choices during that whole window isn't required, since the deficit itself only depends on total energy, not food "cleanliness." States his own typical deficit is just normal eating minus ~400–500 kcal/day, usually via cutting a few incidental snacks, with no change to the actual foods eaten.
- **Sub-mistake B — cutting too aggressively:** as results start showing, there's a temptation to push the deficit harder/faster; but large deficits (he references up to ~1,000 kcal/day as an extreme) can only be sustained briefly before hunger and low energy force a stop — usually before reaching the actual target. Recommends a moderate pace: roughly 0.5 kg (about 1 lb) of fat loss per week once already at a fairly lean starting point.
- Rule of thumb for knowing when to stop a cut: continue at that moderate pace until it starts to become genuinely difficult (increased hunger/lower enjoyment) — that point marks a sustainable, comfortable leanness level for the current amount of muscle mass, without needing to grind through real discomfort to get there.
- Personal example given: a 5-week cut losing roughly 2.5–3 kg, stopped once it started feeling like a mild inconvenience rather than continuing to a harder end point.

## Overall framing
- Presents fat-loss ability as a learnable, repeatable skill (get the deficit right, execute it without needless restriction, stop at the moderate-effort ceiling) rather than a one-time diet to survive — once learned, it's positioned as something that "never gets harder than the first time."
