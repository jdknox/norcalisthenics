# How I stay lean eating as much as I want
2025-11-28

[In this video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nlGZZA_alsY) Woods sums up the outcome after a decade of adjusting his diet: "I eat as much as I want, it's delicious, and I stay lean."

Below is a paraphrased summary, not a transcript.

## Core mechanism
- Frames hunger as driven by two separate needs: total energy and specific essential nutrients (protein especially) — if nutrient needs (particularly protein) aren't met, hunger persists even after energy needs are met, pushing toward overeating regardless of calorie target.
- Claims that once both needs are properly met, appetite regulates itself accurately to the body's actual energy requirement — removing the need to track or calculate calories directly, provided basic strength training is also happening (referenced at the usual ~40 min/week).

## Two governing principles
1. **Base the diet on single-ingredient whole foods** (not 100% of intake, but the bulk of it).
2. **Prioritize protein until the daily target is hit**, before worrying about anything else.

## Protein sources and rough daily numbers (his own example)
- Primary source: red meat (lamb/beef), slow-cooked and portioned — ~500 g (about 1 lb) of meat ≈ 100 g protein, forming the bulk of his first meal of the day.
- Secondary sources across the week: fish/seafood, pork, chicken; eggs (6 eggs ≈ 40 g protein); dairy — cheese, milk (mainly for coffee), and specifically low/no-fat Greek yogurt as a protein-dense option (example cited: one tub ≈ 90 g protein).
- Stated personal daily protein target: 140 g, with 100–120 g typically covered by the first meal alone (meat + cheese), leaving the rest to come incidentally through the day.
- Tracking approach: no app-based calorie tracking — instead keeps a rough mental "ballpark" of protein content in his regular foods, aiming to land within range rather than hitting an exact number.

## Everything else ("the other 20%")
- Remaining calories come primarily from fruit (whatever's seasonal/available), plus honey/dates for extra carbs, and starchy carbs (rice, potatoes) typically at dinner alongside the protein source.
- States roughly 80% of intake follows the whole-food/protein-first structure above, with the remaining ~20% eaten freely for enjoyment (dessert, chocolate, ice cream) with no specific restriction — argued as sustainable specifically because the protein/nutrient base is already covered, so appetite doesn't push toward excess in that remaining portion.
- Uses fats (butter, olive oil) mainly for cooking/flavor on leaner cuts, and seasons liberally (salt, spices, fermented foods, hot sauce) on the reasoning that minimally processed food needs deliberate flavoring, and that food should be both nutritious and enjoyable rather than one at the expense of the other.

## Supplements/performance items mentioned (all stated as optional, not necessary)
- **Creatine** — 5 g each morning; described as improving training consistency/quality session-to-session (fewer "off" days), inexpensive.
- **Coffee** — for focus/enjoyment, self-imposed cutoff around midday to protect sleep.
- **Black tea** — an afternoon routine item, no specific performance claim attached.

## Caveats given
- Notes the leanness achievable through this approach is capped by current muscle mass — states this diet supports but does not itself drive muscle gain (that's attributed to strength training, referenced as the first priority for any goal).
- For rapid/intentional fat loss beyond what this eating pattern naturally produces, states total energy intake still needs deliberate attention (i.e., an intentional calorie deficit) on top of these two principles.
- Notes non-meat-eaters would need to lean more heavily on eggs/dairy (or whey protein, though he personally doesn't use it) to hit protein targets, and states a vegan approach is explicitly outside the scope of this particular method as described.
