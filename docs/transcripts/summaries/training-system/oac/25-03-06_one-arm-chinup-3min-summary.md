# How anyone can achieve a one-arm chinup in 3 minutes a week
2025-03-06

[In this video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Q3dhtlOq8NE) Woods traces his own path: "About five years ago I stopped doing chin-ups, pull-ups, lap pull downs" — switching to this method instead.

Below is a paraphrased summary, not a transcript. This is the dedicated, step-by-step version of the vertical-pull/one-arm chin-up progression referenced throughout his other content — mechanics overlap substantially with his other movement videos, so this summary focuses on the specifics unique to this one.

## Progression and scaling
- Zero-difficulty baseline: rings set low enough that feet reach the floor, using both feet and (if needed) the other hand on the second ring for assistance — full range of motion defined as dead hang to wrist-touching-chest.
- Scaling up: progressively remove foot/other-arm assistance, shifting more bodyweight onto the working arm, using forced negatives (see his dedicated video on that technique) at each point in the range to find and train at genuine failure.

## Dosage
- One continuous set, once per week: alternate arms rep by rep, brief resets allowed between reps but treated as one set overall; stop as soon as it's no longer possible to give full effort (the same "no longer fun" rule used throughout his content).

## Progress tracking — the specific milestone sequence for this movement
- Because unilateral movements (unlike bilateral ones) lack the same intermediate "progression levels," recommends a specific pre-set test each session: 2-arm partial-range chin-up → full-range 2-arm chin-up → partial-range 1-arm negative → full-range 1-arm negative → partial-range 1-arm positive → full 1-arm chin-up.
- Recommends testing this before the main working set each week specifically to get a clean, trackable data point on current strength, separate from the main training.

## Notes specific to this video
- States this approach took him from "a couple of shitty chin-ups" to a full one-arm chin-up.
- Flags overdoing weekly volume as the most common mistake — pushing past the point of enjoyment doesn't just fail to help, it's specifically called out as reducing training quality/intensity and increasing risk of overuse injuries (tendonitis, tennis elbow) in this movement pattern specifically, given how taxing near-maximal single-arm pulling is on the elbow/wrist tendons.
