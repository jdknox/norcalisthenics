# Bulletproof knees – How to build a nordic curl from scratch in 2 minutes a week
2024-10-08

[In this video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6AxkQmf-8lM) Woods introduces the movement: "The Nordic curl is one of only six movements that I train."

Below is a paraphrased summary, not a transcript. This is his fullest dedicated explanation of the Nordic curl mechanics specifically — general principles overlap with his other movement videos, so this summary focuses on the details unique to this one.

## Purpose and credit
- Frames the Nordic curl as the knee-flexion counterpart to single-leg squat (knee-extension) strength — training both together is framed as building complete leg strength.
- Credits Ben Patrick ("Knees Over Toes Guy") for popularizing this movement's athletic benefits (sprint speed, jump height, injury prevention), noting knee flexion is commonly neglected in typical training.
- States he personally has not yet reached a full (strict, no-hands) Nordic curl after a couple of years of training it, but reports steady continued progress — offered as reassurance that visible mastery isn't required to still be making real gains with this method.

## Equipment
- Any anchor point that can hold the heels against upward/backward force — a padded Nordic strap under a door is the simplest option; a loaded barbell in a rack, a fixed squat rack, or a balustrade also work. Pad the knees if training on a hard floor.

## Biomechanics — simplified to one joint
- Frames the entire movement as just a 90°–180° knee flexion/extension arc — explicitly says not to think about what the upper body is doing, just to focus on the knee angle through that range.

## Two difficulty-scaling variables (specific to this movement)
1. **Hip hinge** — bending at the hips shortens the effective lever arm, reducing knee-joint load; this is the first adjustment to make it easier.
2. **Hand support** — once hinged enough to reach the ground, hands can be used to take additional load off the knees, providing further scaling.
- Combining both allows scaling from fully assisted (hips bent, full hand support) down to zero assistance (hips extended, no hands) — covering the entire beginner-to-advanced range with these two variables alone.

## Training method
- Same self-scaled maximal-intensity principle as his other movements: work every degree of the range at genuine failure, forcing the negative (descent) by actively trying to stay up while allowing bodyweight to "pry" the knee open toward the ground.
- Dosage: one rep, roughly 1–2 minutes, once per week; stop once satisfied/no longer wanting to continue.

## Key mistake flagged specifically for this movement
- Training only a partial range (e.g., stopping at ~45° of knee bend and calling it failure) rather than continuing all the way down until the hips touch the floor — states a rep doesn't "count" toward full-range strength gain unless the hips make contact with the ground at the bottom; partial-range training only builds partial-range strength.
