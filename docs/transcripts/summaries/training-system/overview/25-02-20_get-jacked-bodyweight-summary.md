# How to get jacked with bodyweight training (40min/week)
2025-02-20

[In this video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AIEDZJ3K7x8) Woods opens with a blunt diagnosis: "most body weight training, quite frankly is useless, but it doesn't have to be."

Below is a paraphrased summary, not a transcript. Core content (six movements, self-scaled difficulty, once-weekly training) overlaps heavily with his other videos — this summary focuses on the two specific "failure points" framing unique to this one.

## Two stated reasons most bodyweight training fails
1. **No strength goals hard enough to force real change** — most people's bodyweight training targets (basic pushups, pull-ups) aren't demanding enough relative to advanced levels to drive significant muscle/strength adaptation once a base level is reached.
2. **No process for training productively below those goals** — even with a hard end-goal in mind, most people have no way to train *toward* it if they can't yet do it at all (i.e. no scaling method), so the goal stays permanently out of reach.

## The six goals (his "north star" set, detailed further in other summarized videos)
- Straddle planche, front lever, handstand pushup, one-arm chin-up, single-leg squat loaded to 75% bodyweight, and a strict Nordic curl.
- Explains why these specific movements work as targets: they're all strongly dependent on strength-to-bodyweight ratio, meaning achieving them requires both substantial muscle mass and low body fat simultaneously — so reaching them essentially guarantees the physique outcome (lean, muscular) as a side effect, without needing to separately track appearance.
- Personal framing: describes shifting his own approach once he realized how demanding these movements actually were — rather than worrying about how his physique looked or whether he was gaining muscle, he could ignore appearance entirely and track only incremental strength progress toward these movements, trusting that reaching them would mean the physique goal had been met too.

## Training approach (brief — full mechanics in his other videos)
- Approximate the goal movement at current strength level (using assistance/positioning to make it just barely possible), train there at genuine maximal effort, and let the approximation improve over years as strength builds — described as fundamentally a skill-practice process, differing from typical skill learning only in that "getting better" here means physically building muscle tissue.
- One set per movement per week, stopping at the same "no longer fun" cue used throughout his content; total weekly time estimate given here: roughly 12–18 minutes of actual working time.
