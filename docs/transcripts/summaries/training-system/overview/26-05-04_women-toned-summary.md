# Why most women never get toned (and what actually works)
2026-05-04

[In this video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0NIBLo2jgN8) Woods argues the common advice fails because "you're avoiding the one thing that actually creates that look" — muscle.

Below is a paraphrased summary, not a transcript.

## Core framework
- Body composition/appearance is determined by only two adjustable variables: muscle mass and body fat mass.
- Common mistake #1: avoiding muscle-building out of fear of "bulk." Counter-argument: perceived bulkiness is attributed to excess body fat, not muscle; building visible muscle mass as a woman is described as physiologically much harder than for a man (lower testosterone), requiring the same intense, consistent effort for proportionally less muscle gained — so the "risk" of accidentally over-building muscle is characterized as very low.
- Common mistake #2: targeting lower body *weight* rather than lower body *fat* — dropping weight without building/maintaining muscle produces a smaller but undefined ("skinny fat") physique rather than visible tone/shape, and is described as harder to sustain (low body weight relative to a person's natural muscle mass tends to increase hunger and rebound).

## Why muscle mass matters even if the stated goal is fat loss
- More muscle mass allows reaching a lower, leaner body-fat percentage while staying at a healthier/more sustainable overall body weight — producing visible definition/shape rather than just smaller size.
- More muscle also raises basal metabolic rate, meaning more food can be eaten at maintenance once muscle is added — framed as easier long-term adherence, not just a training benefit.

## Protocol
- Strength: the same six-basic-movement system referenced throughout his content — one movement per basic pattern, trained once/week as heavy/hard as possible, ~40 minutes/week total.
- Nutrition — protein target: 1.8 g per kg of goal bodyweight/day (worked example: 60 kg goal bodyweight → 110 g/day target; achievable via ~300 g lean meat + Greek yogurt/cheese/eggs at one meal).
- Fat loss phase: calorie deficit periods, capped at no more than 12 weeks per phase, targeting no faster than ~0.5 kg (about 1 lb)/week loss, followed by stabilizing at the new leaner level rather than continuing to diet indefinitely.

## Notes on body-part shaping
- States that targeted-region shaping (e.g. glutes without shoulders) is not meaningfully controllable through exercise selection — relative size across body regions is attributed to genetics and fat distribution, not which exercises are chosen; developing all major muscle groups (including legs) is described as improving overall leanness/proportion even for people who don't specifically prioritize a given region.
