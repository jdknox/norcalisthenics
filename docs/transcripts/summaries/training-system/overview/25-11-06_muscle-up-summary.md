# This is why you still can't do a muscle up
2025-11-06

[In this video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ixFPYeZqpiw) Woods states there are "only two possible reasons you can't do a muscle up yet" — and practicing muscle-ups isn't the fix for either.

Below is a paraphrased summary, not a transcript.

## Core argument
- The muscle-up is framed as a composite of three ordinary strength requirements (vertical pull, then horizontal pull, then horizontal push), plus one piece of technique (false grip) — not a unique skill requiring dedicated practice.
- Claims that once the underlying strength thresholds are met, the movement itself typically requires no meaningful additional practice — the two blockers are (a) insufficient strength in one of the three component planes, or (b) not knowing the false-grip technique.

## Three stated strength requisites (each pegged to a specific milestone)
1. **Vertical pull** — full range-of-motion two-arm chin-up: from a complete dead hang to wrists touching the chest. This range gets you into position for the transition phase.
2. **Horizontal pull (the "transition")** — full range-of-motion tuck front lever row: elbows getting past the body, i.e. the same shoulder-extension range used in pulling the body from under the rings to over them.
3. **Horizontal push (the "dip")** — tuck planche pushup: enough horizontal pushing strength/range (equivalent range of motion to a bodyweight dip) to push from the bottom "dip" position up to the top of the muscle-up.
- Each is described as roughly a "level 4 of 7" milestone in his broader progression system (see `woods_levels.md`) — intermediate, not advanced; achievable with the same scaled bodyweight training described throughout his content (guiding through the range with feet/legs as needed, extending range over time).
- Notes these three strength standards inherently require a lean-enough body composition as well, since they're all relative-bodyweight movements.

## Technique — false grip
- Necessity: a normal (non-false) grip makes the transition physically impossible without further rotating the wrist around the ring mid-movement.
- False grip = maintaining the same wrist/hand position used at the top (dip) position from the very start of the movement, with the meaty part of the wrist/lower forearm (not the palm) bearing weight against the ring.
- Setup recommendation: wooden gymnastics rings (easier to grip than plastic/metal), chalk on the wrist/forearm contact area for friction.
- Execution: pull up to the top of a chin-up while holding false grip, then continue pulling the elbows back until the chest passes over the hands (the transition) and up into the dip position — from there, dip up to complete the movement.
- Advises minimizing direct false-grip practice specifically because it can abrade the wrist/forearm skin — build strength via the three component movements above, and only use false grip when actually attempting the full muscle-up.
- Full standard/end goal: from a false-grip dead hang ("eagle hang," wrist rotated outward while maintaining false grip), pull continuously through to a fully locked-out ring support at the top.

## Framing
- Positions the muscle-up primarily as a useful checkpoint/byproduct of reaching intermediate strength across the upper-body movement patterns, rather than a goal requiring its own dedicated training block.
