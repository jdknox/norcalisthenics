# How To Get Twice As Strong In Half The Time
2024-11-05

[In this video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ts6LztxL8Io) Woods opens with the claim: "Here's how you can get twice as strong in half the time."

Below is a paraphrased summary, not a transcript. Core content (movement patterns over individual muscles, once-weekly training, progressive overload as the only thing that matters) overlaps heavily with several other summarized videos — this one's distinct contribution is directly arguing against high-frequency training as a default assumption.

## Core argument against high-frequency routines
- Directly targets the common fitness-industry assumption that multiple sessions per muscle group per week are necessary — argues this assumption is what causes most people to set an unsustainable routine (5–6 gym days/week), fail to maintain it, and stay stuck rather than progressing.
- States once-per-week training per movement is "just ample," reiterating that progress depends entirely on continually raising the difficulty over time (progressive overload) for a small set of movement patterns — not on frequency, volume, rep ranges, or rest periods, which he states don't meaningfully matter as long as intensity and consistency are there.

## Addressing the "isn't twice a week more optimal?" objection (specific to this video)
- Response: because his six movements are naturally paired by antagonist pattern (horizontal push+pull, vertical push+pull) and split across different days, upper-body patterns already end up hit roughly twice a week as a side effect of the split — without deliberately designing for higher frequency.
- States he's seen no research that contradicts this incidental frequency being close to optimal anyway, framing the "once per movement per week" default as landing in a good spot even under a frequency-optimization lens.

## Time math specific to this video
- Worst case (no splitting, one big session): ~40 minutes/week including full rest and setup time.
- Best case (split into 3 sessions of 2 movements each): ~30 minutes/week total, ~10 minutes/session.
- Both framed as dramatically less than the 5–6 hours/week a traditional high-frequency program would require — and, per his sustainability argument, more likely to actually be maintained for the years required to reach a goal physique.
