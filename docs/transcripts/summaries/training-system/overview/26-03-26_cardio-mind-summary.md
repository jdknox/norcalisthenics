# Cardio is for your mind, not your body
2026-03-26

[In this video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_dkf-A1Woeo) Woods states plainly: "Cardio in isolation will not change your body, get you leaner or build strength."

Below is a paraphrased summary, not a transcript.

## Core argument
- For physique goals (leaner, more muscular, better-looking), only two levers matter: strength training and diet (daily protein intake + a calorie deficit until lean). Cardio's effect on these outcomes is indirect at best and, past a certain volume, can start working against progress (e.g. high-volume running is described as hard on joints and not conducive to building muscle mass).
- Reframes cardio's actual value as primarily psychological/wellbeing-related, not physical: mood, energy, sleep, focus — separate from strength or physique goals entirely.
- Personal anecdote: noticed a sharp decline in mood/sleep/energy during a period of near-zero incidental movement (2020 lockdown) despite maintaining full strength training — traced the cause to a lack of general movement/walking, not strength training itself.

## Three stated principles for cardio/movement
1. **Do what you enjoy** — the specific activity matters far less than that some movement is happening; pick whatever doesn't feel like a chore (his examples: walking with a podcast/audiobook, basketball, tennis, surfing — explicitly not swimming, which he dislikes).
2. **Let intuition set the amount** — no strict prescription; a rough target of 8,000–10,000 steps/day and/or 60–90 minutes of some physical activity is offered as a ballpark that works well for most people, but the actual guide is doing until it feels sufficient.
3. **Intensity doesn't matter** — for the wellbeing purpose being targeted, low-intensity activity (walking) is sufficient and preferred; this also has practical benefits — minimal impact on recovery (so it doesn't compete with strength training) and it burns energy without significantly increasing appetite, aiding fat-loss efforts as a side effect.

## Practical takeaway
- States his full routine outside strength training is, most days, simply walking (8,000–10,000 steps), done whenever convenient (often morning or after dinner), sometimes social/with audio content, sometimes just for mental space — plus occasional higher-effort activities (tennis, basketball, surfing, golf) when time/opportunity allows, treated as bonus rather than required.
