# The 6 movements you MUST be doing for a great body
2025-01-21

[In this video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7cPHXd6Vf2k) Woods states his track record: "I have used just six movements over the past five years in my training" to transform his physique.

Below is a paraphrased summary, not a transcript. The six-movement list overlaps with several other summarized videos — this one's distinct contribution is the "range of motion / strength curve" argument for *why* six movements suffice, detailed below.

## Core argument — the "strength curve" problem with fixed-resistance exercises
- Any given joint has a large total range of motion (e.g. the shoulder: full extension behind the body to full flexion overhead; the hip: full flexion at the bottom of a squat to full extension standing/jumping).
- A fixed external load (barbell, machine, standard bodyweight exercise) is only maximally challenging at one specific point in that range (the "sticking point," where mechanical disadvantage is greatest) — everywhere else in the range is sub-maximal for that same load, so only a small slice of the total range of motion actually gets trained to true failure.
- Illustrates with a "rainbow" analogy: each fixed exercise only "colors in" one narrow band of a movement pattern's full range; covering the whole range with fixed-resistance exercises would require many different movements (e.g. squat + deadlift + box squat + rack pull, for hip extension alone) — and even then, gaps between them likely remain.

## Proposed solution
- Because bodyweight-leverage training allows difficulty to be continuously adjusted through body positioning, the difficulty can be matched to the body's natural "strength curve" throughout an entire rep — making every portion of the range of motion maximally challenging, not just one sticking point.
- Result: a single self-scaled movement can cover the effective training benefit of several different fixed-resistance exercises stacked together, because the whole range gets trained to failure rather than just a slice of it.

## Mapping this to four upper-body + two lower-body movements
- Upper body: full shoulder range requires one movement pair for extension→mid-flexion and another for mid-flexion→full flexion, in each direction — covered by horizontal push (planche pushup) + vertical push (handstand pushup) for the pushing direction, and horizontal pull (front lever row) + vertical pull (one-arm chin-up) for the pulling direction.
- Lower body: hip flexion→extension covered by the single-leg squat (loaded to ~75% bodyweight); explicitly states he hasn't found an equally effective self-scaled movement for the reverse (hip flexion) pattern, and instead substitutes direct knee-flexion training (Nordic curl) as the best available option for the posterior chain — citing Ben Patrick ("Knees Over Toes Guy") as doing more dedicated work on hip-flexion-specific training that he hasn't personally adopted.

## Stated implication
- Because each of the six movements, trained this way, already covers its entire relevant range of motion, no additional isolation, mobility, or "weak link" work is needed — describes his own past experience with extensive supplementary work (scapular retraction drills, isolation exercises, mobility "bulletproofing") as unnecessary once this six-movement full-range approach replaced it, with calisthenics skills (levers, handstand presses) emerging automatically once the underlying strength was built.
