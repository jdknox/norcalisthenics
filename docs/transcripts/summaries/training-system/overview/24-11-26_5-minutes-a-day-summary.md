# The New Way To Build Muscle In 5 Minutes A Day
2024-11-26

[In this video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eKt66H2KRAs) Woods opens with the framing: "This is the new way to build muscle in about 5 minutes a day."

Below is a paraphrased summary, not a transcript. Core content (six movements, self-scaled max-intensity training, once-weekly sets) overlaps heavily with many other summarized videos — this one's distinct contribution is framed around long-term/lifetime sustainability rather than short-term results.

## Distinct framing — "maintenance is a myth"
- Central argument specific to this video: since muscle/strength training results take years regardless of method, and most people want to stay fit into old age (not just for a few months), the deciding factor in lifetime outcome is whether a routine can be sustained indefinitely — not how optimal it is in the short term.
- Explicitly states that "maintaining" isn't a real steady state for the human body — without continued stimulus a person is always trending toward loss of strength/mobility, not staying flat — so a sustainable-forever routine is framed as a necessity, not a nice-to-have.
- Argues that even a training method that left 10–20% of theoretical gains on the table would still be worth using if it were the difference between a person training for 5 years vs. quitting after 5 months — sustainability outweighs marginal optimization.

## Protocol (same as his other content, briefly)
- Six movements (straddle planche, front lever, handstand pushup, one-arm chin-up, single-leg squat at 75% bodyweight, Nordic curl), each trained once/week at self-scaled maximum intensity from the first centimeter of the rep, forcing negatives at the top rather than lowering under control.
- Stopping rule: same "no longer fun" cue used throughout his content; states most of his own sets land in the 1–3 minute range.
- Notes this can be split as one movement per day (6 days/week, ~5 min/day) if that structure suits someone better than grouping movements into fewer, longer sessions — offered here as an explicit alternative split not emphasized elsewhere.
