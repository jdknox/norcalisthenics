# How to maximise muscle gain in 40 minutes a week
2024-12-19

[In this video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UDyAqBfnKXA) Woods opens with the promise: "I'm going to show you right now how you can maximise muscle gain."

Below is a paraphrased summary, not a transcript. Core content (mechanical tension, self-scaled training, minimal effective volume) overlaps heavily with his other videos — this one's distinct contribution is a specific quantified breakdown of how little of a traditional workout is genuinely effective.

## Core argument, quantified
- Identifies mechanical tension (muscles working at/near their absolute force limit) as the fundamental driver of muscle growth.
- Walks through a specific estimate for a traditional gym session: assuming ~20 sets in a 60-minute session, each containing roughly 3 seconds of genuine near-failure effort, that totals about 1 minute of truly maximal-intensity work across the entire hour — under 2% of total session time.
- Frames the rest of a traditional session (warm-up sets, sub-maximal reps building toward failure) as contributing little to muscle growth directly, while still accumulating fatigue and joint/connective-tissue stress — explicitly calls this "the most sinister" category of training, since it produces cost (fatigue, injury risk) without the corresponding growth benefit.

## The "sniper vs. automatic rifle" analogy (specific to this video)
- Traditional fixed-resistance training compared to firing an automatic rifle without aiming: many "shots" (reps/sets) are taken, a few incidentally land on the target (moments of true failure), but most miss (sub-maximal work) — using a large volume of "ammunition" (time, energy, joint wear) for a small amount of actual signal.
- Self-scaled, continuously-adjusted training compared to a sniper taking one well-aimed shot: cutting out everything except the genuinely maximal-effort work, hitting the same target with far less total input.

## Reiterated numbers
- Claims constant-max-intensity training across his six basic movements can reach the same weekly muscle-growth stimulus as the "1 minute" of effective traditional-training time above, in roughly 20 minutes/week of dedicated body-weight training (up to ~40 min/week including rest) — the discrepancy explained by his training containing a much higher proportion of genuinely maximal-effort time per minute spent, rather than an equal amount of "good" stimulus condensed.
- Notes diminishing returns and downsides (lower training quality, higher injury/tendon-strain risk, no additional growth) begin quickly past roughly 2–3 minutes of this style of work per movement — consistent with figures given throughout his other content.

## Notes specific to this video
- Explicitly reassures that freed-up training time is meant to be spent on anything else enjoyable (sport, cardio, hobbies, rest) rather than viewed as "wasted" potential training time — frames strength training as purely a tool for eliciting a growth signal, not an activity with inherent value beyond that signal.
