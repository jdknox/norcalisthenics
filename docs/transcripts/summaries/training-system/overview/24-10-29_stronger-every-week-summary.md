# How to get stronger every single week (WITHOUT training more)
2024-10-29

[In this video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Whq1BGX21M8) Woods opens with: "Here's how you can start getting stronger every single week, regardless of your current" rate of progress.

Below is a paraphrased summary, not a transcript. Core content (six movements, minimal volume, protein target) overlaps heavily with other summarized videos — this one's distinct contribution is framing three specific "barriers" to signal-sending plus two specific recovery factors, as a diagnostic checklist for people who are stuck.

## Three barriers to effective signal-sending (the specific list in this video)
1. **Too much weekly volume** — spreading effort across many sets/hours makes it physiologically impossible to keep every rep at genuine maximal intensity; diluted intensity across a long session is framed as strictly worse than less time at true maximal effort.
2. **Too many exercises** — isolating individual muscles from many angles (his example: multiple different lateral raise variations) scatters focus across too many things to push meaningful progress on any of them; recommends collapsing to one movement per basic pattern (six total) so all effort channels into six trackable progress lines.
3. **No clear, sufficiently hard strength goals** — without a specific, multi-year-difficulty target per movement, training has no direction, and (per his framing) people naturally default to the easiest version of "showing up," which doesn't produce real progress.

## Two recovery factors (the specific list in this video)
1. **Protein** — reiterates the 1.8 g/kg target (his example: 150 g/day at 80 kg) as the primary lever; states that with protein and training both handled, most people don't need to separately worry about total calorie intake, since appetite regulation handles it.
2. **Sleep and downtime** — states that because actual training time is minimal (his estimate: ~30 min/week), the much larger remaining time budget for recovery (sleep, rest, daily downtime) is what does the actual work of building muscle — framed as equally important to the training signal itself, just easier to get right since it mostly just requires not interfering with it.

## Framing
- Positions all five factors (3 barriers + 2 recovery items) as things to *remove* or *simplify*, not things to add — consistent with the "eliminate obstacles rather than add more work" theme running through his broader content.
