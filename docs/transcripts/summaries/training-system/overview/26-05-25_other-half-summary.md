# I only train 40 mins/week (here's the other half)
2026-05-25

[In this video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3VKfjWB4Tas) Woods frames it simply: "Between these two sessions, this is everything I do to build my physique."

Below is a paraphrased summary, not a transcript. This is a workout walkthrough video (companion to "here's half," summarized separately) covering vertical push, vertical pull, and the remaining leg work.

## Strength testing vs. training within a session
- For movements with a clear ceiling goal (e.g. one-arm chin-up, handstand pushup), the session starts with a strength test at that ceiling movement while fresh — this both trains and produces a measurable data point ("frontier") for that session, distinct from working through full-range reps at a lower progression.
- Rationale for testing at a fixed bodyweight-relative goal: because these are bodyweight movements, raw capability (e.g. having done a one-arm chin-up before) can be masked or unlocked purely by bodyweight changes — so bodyweight needs to be tracked alongside strength to interpret progress correctly (relative strength, not just capability, is the real measure).

## Training volume clarification
- Addresses a common misconception that 40 min/week implies low volume: because every second of work shown is performed at/near failure, it's the effective-volume equivalent of many traditional sets taken to failure with a barbell.
- With barbell/fixed-resistance training, adding volume requires adding whole rest-separated sets; with self-scaled bodyweight training at constant near-max effort, volume is instead added by simply continuing the same set longer.
- Rule of thumb for how much volume/work to do: continue as long as you can sustain genuinely maximal effort output; stop once you can no longer produce that same peak intensity — additional work below that intensity doesn't add benefit and just accumulates fatigue/wear.

## Recent aggressive fat-loss note

## Leg training on the road (equipment-limited alternative)
- Normally uses a weighted vest for loaded single-leg squats; while traveling without one, substituted two 30 kg dumbbells held at the sides for equivalent external load.
- Noted trade-off: dumbbells pull the body down deeper at the bottom of the movement (a plus), but lose the side-to-side balance assistance a vest + hand support on a platform provides.
- Mentions a Smith Machine as a likely good gym-based alternative for this pattern (detailed separately elsewhere in his content).
