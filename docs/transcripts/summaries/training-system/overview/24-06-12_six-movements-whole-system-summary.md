# The six (or five) movements as the entire training answer
Three overlapping posts/videos, combined here since they cover the same core argument from different angles:
- "What exercise do you need to get in great shape?" — 2024-06-27 — https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YVxCx4YXeDs
- "How I built my dream body in 40 minutes a week" — 2024-06-12 — https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6W5G0UzTzVc
- "Why You Only Need 5 Exercises For An Elite Physique" — 2023-08-23 (blog post; demo video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ap4U0qfwCQE)

Below is a paraphrased summary, not a transcript, of the shared argument across all three, with notes on what's distinct to each. Core content (the six movements, once-weekly max-intensity training, protein target) overlaps heavily with many other summarized videos in this collection — this file focuses on the specific framing unique to these three.

## The shared core argument
- **Exercise has exactly one purpose:** triggering muscle growth. Appearance (lean, toned, defined) is a function of two variables only — muscle mass (from training) and body fat (from diet/energy balance) — so training's entire job is maximizing the first variable, nothing else.
- **"Muscle = strength":** rejects any meaningful distinction between "hypertrophy training" and "strength training" — beyond early neurological/skill adaptation, the only way to keep getting stronger long-term is by building more muscle tissue, so measurable strength gain and muscle gain are treated as the same underlying process.
- **The movement-pattern logic:** because there are only so many planes of motion the body can move through (horizontal push/pull, vertical push/pull, squat, and its opposite), selecting one sufficiently hard strength goal per plane and training only those is argued to guarantee comprehensive muscle development — no need for isolation work, targeted "weak link" training, or a long exercise list.
- **The point of picking very hard goals specifically:** movements like a straddle planche, front lever, handstand pushup, one-arm chin-up, and a heavily loaded single-leg squat require both high absolute strength and low body fat to perform at all — so progressing toward them mechanically guarantees the physique outcome (lean + muscular) as a side effect, without needing to separately track appearance.

## What's distinct to each post
- **"What exercise do you need to get in great shape?"** is the cleanest statement of the "only two variables" argument (muscle from training, fat from diet) and explicitly separates his core six-movement routine from "incidental" exercise (daily walking, occasional social sport) — framing the latter as done purely for enjoyment/mental health, with zero bearing on the physique outcome.
- **"How I built my dream body in 40 minutes a week"** is his fullest single-post walkthrough combining all three pillars together: (1) training — the six movements, once/week, max effort, stop when no longer fun; (2) nutrition — 1.8 g/kg protein target (his example: ~140 g/day at ~80 kg), mostly from one meal, free eating the rest of the day; (3) fat-loss strategy — calorie deficits run in short (4–12 week) bursts, stopped once genuinely difficult, followed by a return to eating-to-appetite and muscle building, with another cut revisited roughly every ~9 months if further leanness is wanted. Frames the whole system as a choice between two paths: complicated/willpower-dependent training and dieting (Option 1) vs. a routine simple enough that there's no real reason not to sustain it for decades (Option 2).
- **"Why you only need 5 exercises"** uses a 5-movement version (omitting the Nordic curl found in his more complete 6-movement system elsewhere) — notes explicitly that this leaves knee flexion (and hip/ankle flexion) untrained, but argues a single-leg squat alone is sufficient for most people's lower-body transformation goals; credits Ben Patrick ("Kneesovertoesguy") as a resource for anyone wanting to specifically address hip/ankle flexion, which he doesn't personally train.
