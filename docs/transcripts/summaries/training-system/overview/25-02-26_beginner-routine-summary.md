# The best beginner training routine – zero to strong in 40min/week
2025-02-26

[In this video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mYb3sgft9Dw) Woods addresses beginners directly: "If you're a beginner, you can't start with the end goal stuff."

Below is a paraphrased summary, not a transcript. This is the dedicated beginner-focused walkthrough of finding "zero difficulty" for all six movements — the underlying principle overlaps with his other content, so this summary focuses on the beginner-specific framing and the zero-scaling detail for each movement in one place.

## Core message for beginners
- States the exact same training principle applies regardless of starting strength — the only difference between an advanced trainee and a complete beginner is the absolute difficulty level trained at, not the method.
- Key reassurance: every movement in his six-movement system can be scaled all the way down to zero required strength, so there's no strength prerequisite to start — cites his mother (early 60s, untrained for years, knee replacement, fused ankle) successfully training at a fully scaled-down level as a concrete example.

## Finding "zero" for each of the six movements (full range of motion, no load on working muscles)
- **Horizontal push (planche pushup):** feet under the body, knee/foot brought in close enough that all weight sits on the feet — a standard pushup with effectively no hand load.
- **Horizontal pull (front lever row):** feet walked in toward the body until weight is off the hands entirely — a standing-assisted row.
- **Vertical push (handstand pushup):** feet brought down from any elevated position to the floor (a "pike" position) so weight is fully on the feet.
- **Vertical pull (one-arm chin-up):** other hand on the second ring, feet on the ground, guiding through a squat-assisted pull with no real load on the working arm.
- **Squat (single-leg squat):** weight shifted onto the other leg and both hands on support platforms, working leg contributing minimal/no force.
- **Knee flexion (Nordic curl):** hips bent to shorten the lever, hands on the ground for full support.
- In each case, the standard is a complete, full-range-of-motion rep achievable with no perceptible effort from the target muscles — this is the starting point before any difficulty is added.

## Scaling up from zero
- Once zero is established, difficulty is added gradually (more forward lean, less assistance, added external weight, etc. — mechanics specific to each movement, covered in his dedicated per-movement videos) until reaching the point of failure — using the "hold at the top, increase difficulty until forced down" method (his forced-negatives technique, covered in a separately summarized video) as the recommended way to reliably find that limit.

## Protocol
- One set per movement, once per week, split across the week however is convenient; roughly 1–3 minutes of actual working time per movement, stopping once no longer enjoyable/sustainable at full effort.
- Reiterates that the specific numbers game (40 min/week including rest) is a generous estimate — actual working time across all six movements can be closer to 12–20 minutes.
