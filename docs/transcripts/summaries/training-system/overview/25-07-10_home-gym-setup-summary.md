# Everything you need to train at home forever (full $500 setup)
2025-07-10

[In this video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WXJXgzTFmAE) Woods sets the bar low: "If you have a hundred bucks and can spare 40 minutes a week" you have what's needed to start.

Below is a paraphrased summary, not a transcript. All prices as stated by Woods (Australian dollars unless noted) — treat as illustrative, not current pricing.

## Three-tier equipment priority
### Tier 1 — Gymnastics rings (covers the entire upper body, for life)
- Positioned as the single highest-value purchase: scales from absolute beginner (assisted pushups, rows, feet-assisted chin-ups) all the way to advanced (levers, planche pushups, one-arm chin-ups) with no other equipment needed for the upper body.
- Preference: wooden rings, 28mm diameter (a matter of personal feel), with a buckle-style strap (adjustable length, doesn't require a fixed anchor loop) over carabiner-style straps.
- Cost range cited: roughly $30 (basic, e.g. plastic, off Amazon) up to ~$120 AUD (his preferred wooden set).
- Anchor options, in order of accessibility: a doorway chin-up bar (~$50 AUD, works but limits vertical space); a freestanding chin-up bar/squat rack; a bolted overhead anchor point into a ceiling beam (most space, described as his home setup) — any sufficiently sturdy overhead point works, including outdoor structures/trees when traveling.

### Tier 2 — Nordic curl strap + elevated platforms (~$30, plus items already on hand)
- Nordic curl anchor: a padded strap that wedges under a closed door (~$30 AUD on Amazon); knees padded with a folded towel. A dedicated Nordic bench or a sturdy railing are optional upgrades, not necessary.
- Elevated support platforms (for single-leg squat assistance and some upper-body progressions): any sturdy furniture at roughly mid-thigh-to-hip height works — bar stools, boxes, etc. — no specific product needed.

### Tier 3 — Loaded weight for single-leg squats (~$340 AUD, the most expensive tier)
- Needed once bodyweight single-leg squats are no longer challenging enough.
- Recommends two 20 kg weight vests (stacked for 40 kg starting load, with room to add a third later) rather than a single large vest.
- No-cost/travel alternative: a training partner (ideally ~40–60 kg) sitting on the shoulders in place of external weight.

## Budget configurations given
- **Minimum budget (well under $100):** any pair of rings (wooden preferred) + a cheap doorway chin-up bar + a $30 Nordic strap + furniture already on hand for platforms. No weight vests yet.
- **Recommended full setup (~$490 AUD):** premium wooden 28mm rings (~$140) + a proper overhead anchor point + Nordic strap (~$30) + two 20 kg weight vests (~$340).
- **Higher-end optional upgrades:** a dedicated Nordic bench instead of the strap, a third weight vest purchased upfront, and/or purpose-built wooden plyo boxes instead of improvised furniture — framed as nice-to-haves, not necessary for results.

## Notes
- States parallettes (used in some of his own demo videos for visual clarity) provide no unique training benefit over rings and aren't necessary — an optional, low-priority extra.
- General framing: equipment doesn't need to be complete or ideal before starting — begin with whatever is available and upgrade later as budget/space allow.
