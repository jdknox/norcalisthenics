# How to get an athletic body fast (40 minutes a week)
2025-01-14

[In this video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LEFiJMtz8ME) Woods opens with the pitch: "I'm going to show you right now how you can transform your body."

Below is a paraphrased summary, not a transcript. Core content (physiological limits on muscle gain/fat loss, minimal effective training, protein target) overlaps heavily with several other summarized videos — this one's distinct contribution is the "boiling water" analogy and its specific framing of consistency as the primary lever.

## Core argument
- States physique change is governed by only two variables (muscle mass, body fat), and that both have a physiological upper limit on how fast they can change per week regardless of effort — meaning once training and nutrition are dialed to hit that limit, the only way to speed up *overall* results is to never fall below it (i.e., be perfectly consistent), not to try to exceed it.

## The "boiling water" analogy (central framing device of this video)
- Describes training/nutrition consistency like keeping a pot of water at a rolling boil: once it reaches boiling (i.e., the effective weekly stimulus for muscle growth or the target calorie deficit), any additional heat (extra training, extra deficit) is wasted — it doesn't cook the food faster, it just uses more energy for no gain.
- The practical goal, per this framing: find the minimum effort that reaches "boiling" and maintain exactly that — not more, since more doesn't help, and not less, since less means the process runs slower or stalls.
- Extends the analogy to describe why traditional high-effort fitness routines fail long-term: they aim for a rolling boil but with far more "heat" (time/effort) than necessary, which makes the routine harder to sustain — so people fall below boiling (miss sessions, quit) more often than someone running an easier, minimal-heat routine that's simple to maintain continuously.

## Numbers reiterated in this video
- Training: constant max-intensity work across the six basic movements, ~20 minutes/week of actual working time (up to ~40 min including rest), is described as already at the "boiling point" for muscle-growth signaling — more doesn't produce additional growth.
- Protein: 1.8 g/kg goal bodyweight/day (his example: ~140 g at ~78 kg), typically via one ~100 g-protein meal — again framed as the "boiling point" past which extra protein yields no additional benefit.
- Fat loss: roughly 0.5 kg/week loss cited as the sustainable "boiling point" for calorie deficit — faster rates are framed as cutting into muscle tissue rather than accelerating fat loss.

## Personal anecdote specific to this video
- Describes friends who tried a high-effort traditional bulking approach (6 days/week gym, mass-gainer shakes) and burned out within a few months, contrasted with people who switched to his minimal system and sustained it for 9–12 months with better results — used to illustrate the core "sustainability beats intensity" argument of the boiling-water framing.
