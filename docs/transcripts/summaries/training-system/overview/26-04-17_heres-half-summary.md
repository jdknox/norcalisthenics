# I only train for 40 minutes a week (here's half)
2026-04-17

[In this video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dbL49fJR4EY) Woods walks through "one of the two 15 minute workouts" making up his weekly training.

Below is a paraphrased summary, not a transcript. Companion video to "here's the other half," summarized separately; this one covers horizontal push, horizontal pull, and squat pattern.

## Exercise selection logic
- Rationale for using only 2 pushing movements (and by extension only 6 total movements): a given muscle (his example: the chest/pec) responds to being asked to get stronger at its function, not to the number of different exercises or accessory variety targeting it — so additional exercise variety for the same movement pattern is treated as redundant rather than additive.
- Equipment: gymnastics rings (portable, fits in a backpack) used for both horizontal and vertical pushing work.

## Rest-within-a-set structure
- Explicitly allows brief rests *between reps within* a single set (rather than treating each rep as fully continuous), reasoning that this makes it easier to maintain full range of motion and "get the most" out of the set.
- Caveats this against overthinking when to stop: keep rest short enough that stopping feels obvious once you no longer want to continue, rather than deliberating over "have I done enough."
- Example set duration given: a horizontal-pushing set run for "over three minutes" when it was the day's training priority.
- General rule: start the session with whichever movement you most want to push progress on, since more time/energy naturally goes to whichever movement comes first.

## Body composition context given
- Describes training this particular session while ~7 weeks into a relaxed travel period (regular eating/drinking, no active fat-loss focus), followed later by a short (~2-week) mini-cut to reset body fat back to a preferred level.
- General point made: once a reasonable base of muscle and consistent protein-first eating is established, moderate indulgence over weeks has limited visible impact on physique, and leanness can be reliably regained on demand via a short deficit — used to argue against needing to stay lean year-round without breaks.

## Squat-pattern travel adaptation
- Normal method: single-leg squats loaded with a weighted vest (~60 kg used elsewhere in his content), typically only 1–2 reps per leg per week.
- Travel substitution (no vest packed): two 30 kg dumbbells held at the sides for equivalent loading — noted to pull the body deeper into the bottom position and feel easier to balance than the vest version in some ways, but loses the side-to-side stability/support that hands-on-a-platform + vest normally provides.
- Mentions testing a Smith Machine version as a potential preferred gym-based alternative (addressed in more detail elsewhere in his content).

## Session numbers given
- Stated total active working time for this half of the week: 6 min 30 sec; total gym visit time: 20 minutes — doubling to match his overall ~40 min/week total across both halves.
