# Beginner to advanced physique – The fastest route (40min a week)
2024-12-03

[In this video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0NA-Ob4IT2E) Woods opens with a clean slate: "Forget everything you know about fitness. If you want a body that looks incredible" and is also functional, here's the sequence.

Below is a paraphrased summary, not a transcript. This video is a condensed three-step overview of his whole system — each step is covered in far more depth in other summarized videos (six-movement training, protein targets, calorie-deficit cycling). This summary captures only the sequencing/priority framing specific to this one.

## Core framing — effectiveness AND sustainability, in a specific order
- Argues most fitness approaches fail on one of two axes: either they don't actually work, or they aren't sustainable enough to stick with long enough to matter — and that both need to be solved together, not just one.
- Presents three steps to be nailed **in sequence**, each with an "effective" requirement and a "sustainable" requirement:

### Step 1 — Muscle mass (training)
- Effective: training hard enough to force strength gain (self-scaled bodyweight training at constant max intensity, six basic movements).
- Sustainable: capped at ~40 minutes/week total, split across 3 short sessions (e.g. two movements, ~10 minutes, three times/week) — framed as the ceiling above which sustainability breaks down for most people.

### Step 2 — Diet (protein)
- Effective: hitting a daily protein target (1.8 g/kg goal bodyweight/day).
- Sustainable: getting the bulk of it (100–120 g) in one meal (his example: lunch) from foods that are actually enjoyable, leaving the rest of the day's eating completely unrestricted — explicitly rejects spreading protein evenly across multiple meals or timing it around workouts as unnecessary friction.

### Step 3 — Fat loss (calorie deficit)
- Effective: a consistent, moderate calorie deficit (~500 kcal/day, ~0.5 kg/week loss).
- Sustainable: simple enough to run as a repeatable daily plan without constant tracking/weighing, and without eliminating social eating, drinking, or treats — monitored via weekly-average body weight rather than obsessive calorie counting; capped at 4–12 week cycles with breaks as needed.

## Stated overall message
- Frames all three steps as individually simple, and states that slow progress is virtually always attributable to one of the three not being properly in place or not being done consistently — rather than to needing additional strategies beyond these three.
- Explicit closing advice: "don't look to do more work... figure out how you can do less and just do it better" — reiterating the minimal-effective-dose theme that runs through his broader content.
