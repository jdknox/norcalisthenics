# I've trained once a week for 6 years. Here's what's missing.
2026-07-13

[In this video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LOFGPLu4WXI) Woods states plainly: "I have only trained six exercises each once per week" for six years.

Below is a paraphrased summary, not a transcript.

## Background
- Cites muscle protein synthesis (MPS) as roughly peaking 24–48 hours after a session and returning to baseline by ~72 hours — meaning under a once-weekly schedule, growth signaling from a given movement is inactive for roughly half the week (his "factory" analogy: production line either running or idle).
- Counterpoint: raising frequency raises accumulated fatigue and injury risk (muscle, tendon, connective tissue), so frequency gains must be balanced against total workload management.
- Rule of thumb offered: aim to hit each muscle ~2x/week (roughly every 3–4 days) as a reasonable middle ground.

## The gap in his existing routine
- His 3-day split already hits upper-body push and pull twice a week each (since horizontal and vertical patterns land on different days).
- Each leg pattern (knee extension, knee flexion) was hit only once a week — identified as the actual gap.

## New experimental split
- Simple A/B split, alternating every training day regardless of calendar day (e.g. Mon A, Wed B, Fri A, next Mon B…), 3 sessions/week, 3 exercises per session (one push, one pull, one leg movement):
  - **Workout A:** horizontal push + horizontal pull + squat pattern
  - **Workout B:** vertical push + vertical pull + Nordic curl
- Net effect: each upper-body pattern trained ~3x/week; each leg pattern ~1.5x/week (a 50% frequency increase over the old once-weekly baseline).

## Time cost
- At 1 set/movement, ~1 minute of work with ~5 minutes rest between different movements: ~13 min/session → ~40 min/week total (roughly unchanged from the original routine).
- Using his separately-described shorter-rest, multi-set protocol (~1 minute rest, each movement repeated for 3 sets): sessions ran ~15–20 minutes → ~45–60 min/week, for the added frequency plus triple the effective sets.

## Caveats
- Explicitly framed as an add-on only for people already consistent and progressing on the once-weekly basics — not a prerequisite.
- States consistency and training intensity (pushing toward genuine progressive overload each session) matter more than this kind of frequency optimization.
