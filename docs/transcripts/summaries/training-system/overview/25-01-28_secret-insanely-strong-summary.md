# The secret to getting insanely strong (it's not what you think)
2025-01-28

[In this video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SM6KXxlXCy0) Woods frames the promise: "I'm going to share with you the secret to getting insanely strong."

Below is a paraphrased summary, not a transcript. Core content (train at strength threshold, self-scaled difficulty, once-weekly sets) overlaps heavily with his other videos — this summary focuses on the analogies and framing specific to this one.

## Core claim
- The single distinguishing factor between people who get strong and people who train for years without results: whether training actually reaches the current threshold of maximum strength, versus staying within a comfortable, sub-maximal range (his example: flexing an arm to brush teeth doesn't build biceps, because it's nowhere near the arm's strength limit).
- States the body only overcompensates (builds strength/muscle beyond baseline) when genuinely pushed to its current limit — anything short of that just maintains the status quo.

## Analogies used specifically in this video
- **Sprinting vs. jogging:** effective training should feel like an all-out sprint (max effort, short duration) rather than the "light jog" most gym training actually amounts to relative to a person's true capacity.
- **Piano practice:** compares training only the genuinely difficult portion of a movement (repeatedly, until mastered) to a musician isolating and drilling only the hard few bars of a piece, rather than replaying the whole song end-to-end to get occasional exposure to the hard part.
- **The "hammer game" at a fair:** describes the intended level of intent for each rep as swinging a sledgehammer to try to beat your own personal-best score — full, maximal effort on every attempt, not a measured or controlled output.

## Framing specific to this video
- Describes his own realization that traditional "good programming" (specific rep/set schemes, periodization) mattered far less than he'd assumed — once training was reduced to one continuous, fully self-scaled set per movement per week, he reports this shift alone (not programming refinement) being what changed his results.
- States that visibly straining facial expressions/effort during training sessions are, in his experience coaching others, a reliable indicator that someone is training at genuine intensity — used as an informal check for whether a student is actually reaching their limit.
- Reiterates that having clearly defined strength goals (the six-movement system covered in his other videos) makes it easier to know, at any given session, roughly how far off the true limit a person's current training is — since testing directly against the goal movement (and failing) reveals the current threshold to train at.
