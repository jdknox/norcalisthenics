# Why most of your training does nothing
2026-08-10

[In this video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DUSU7gylrFU) Woods sums up the core problem: "Zero times a hundred is still zero."

Below is a paraphrased summary, not a transcript.

## Core argument
- Returns on training time are highly non-linear: most activities give close to zero return regardless of volume, while a small subset gives disproportionate results — doing more of a low-yield activity doesn't fix that.
- His own routine has shrunk to roughly 1/10th of its original time investment over several years, as he progressively cut low-yield work.

## What he cut, and why
- **Flexibility/mobility work:** dropped once target ranges were reached — found that once mobility is built, it's easily regained later without ongoing maintenance, so continuing the work felt like unnecessary upkeep rather than progress.
- **Skills practice** (handstands, movement flow, balance/coordination work): dropped once he realized further progress in these was bottlenecked by raw strength rather than skill/technique — so he redirected that time into strength training instead, on the reasoning that strength was the actual limiting factor.
- **~20-minute prehab/warm-up/joint-prep routine:** dropped once fully healthy and training with proper load management, full range of motion, and good recovery — the specificity of the strength training itself was judged to cover what the warm-up was trying to provide. He notes this kind of prep work still has a place for complete beginners easing into training.

## Within strength training itself
- Further distinguishes low-yield strength work: movements that use less total muscle mass, cover a smaller range of motion, or aren't trained with genuine progressive overload (i.e. not pushed close to current max effort each session).
- End state: nearly all training time now goes to multi-joint, large-range-of-motion, maximal-effort compound strength movements; static holds, isolation work, and balance-dependent movements were dropped.
- Explicit warning: training volume beyond what recovery capacity allows moves into *negative* return territory — it actively reduces progress, not just wastes time.
