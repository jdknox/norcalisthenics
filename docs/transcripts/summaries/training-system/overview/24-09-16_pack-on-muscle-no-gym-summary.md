# How to pack on muscle fast (no gym required)
2024-09-16

[In this video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BJBRML3HeLs) Woods states his results: "By using some smart bodyweight training, I've gained about 10kg of lean muscle" in 5–6 years, entirely at home.

Below is a paraphrased summary, not a transcript. Core content (six movements, self-scaled intensity, strength-equals-muscle) overlaps heavily with several other summarized videos — this one's distinct structure is framed as debunking three specific "myths" that kept him tied to gym-based training.

## Myth 1 — "You need weights/machines to build muscle"
- Counter-argument: muscle growth responds to relative intensity (force demand relative to current capacity), not absolute external load — bodyweight leverage alone can generate maximal force demand.
- States fixed-resistance training is actually *less* efficient at reaching true 100%-intensity work across a whole session (most of a gym session stays sub-maximal), whereas self-scaled bodyweight training can be kept at genuine failure continuously.

## Myth 2 — "You need to target every muscle individually"
- Counter-argument: only six basic movement patterns exist to meaningfully train; loading each to maximum capacity through full range of motion is stated to build all supporting/weak-link muscles automatically, without needing separate isolation exercises.
- Adds a specific claim not emphasized elsewhere in his content: cites the distinction between closed-chain movements (moving your body through space, as in bodyweight training) and open-chain movements (moving external weight relative to a fixed body, as with most machines/isolation exercises) — asserts closed-chain training produces more transferable real-world strength.

## Myth 3 — "Muscle growth (hypertrophy) and strength gain are different things, and bodyweight training only gets you 'ripped,' not 'big'"
- Counter-argument: beyond initial neurological/skill adaptations, the only way to continue getting stronger on a given movement long-term is by building more muscle tissue — so measurable strength progress and muscle growth are treated as functionally the same process, not separate goals requiring separate training styles.
- Frames appearance-focused training cues (chasing a "pump," soreness, or a hard workout feeling) as a common trap — argues these don't reliably indicate growth, whereas tracking objective strength progress against defined goals does.

## Practical takeaway (this video's specific 3-step distillation)
1. Stop thinking in terms of external weight — think in terms of force/difficulty demanded, which bodyweight positioning can supply.
2. Stop thinking in terms of individual muscles — think in terms of the six basic movement patterns.
3. Stop chasing hypertrophy-specific training feelings — just focus on getting objectively stronger on those six movements over time.

## Goals referenced in this video
- Straddle planche pushup, front lever row, handstand pushup, one-arm chin-up, single-leg squat +60 kg, Nordic curl — noted here as close to (though not identically worded as) the "75% bodyweight" squat figure and plain "front lever" phrasing used in some of his other videos, reflecting minor wording differences across his content over time.
