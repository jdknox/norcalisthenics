# If you only do one exercise, make it the squat
2026-08-31

[In this video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UwX-MxmDhYg) Woods calls the squat the exercise with "the biggest impact on your physique, mobility, and quality of life."

Below is a paraphrased summary, not a transcript.

## Core principle
- Full range of motion is the key requirement: compress fully at the bottom (knee to chest, hip and ankle maximally flexed) and drive out of that position.
- Load must be heavy enough that the *entire* range is challenging — too light and only the bottom feels hard, which under-stimulates the rest of the range and wastes most of the movement's benefit.
- Compression at the bottom is framed as building ankle/hip/knee mobility automatically, without separate mobility work — most people's limiting joint is ankle flexion.

## Dosage
- ~1 hard set per week (he's currently doing it roughly every second workout, i.e. ~3x/fortnight), alternating legs.
- Additional sets optional; needs little rest between sets compared to normal strength training.

## Variations (in his order of preference)
1. **Loaded single-leg (pistol) squat** — most specific: builds both the strength and the movement pattern/coordination directly. For limited ankle mobility, start with the heel elevated. Load as heavy as controllable, spotting with hands as needed to offload some bodyweight.
2. **Smith Machine single-leg squat** — example load used: 60 kg on the bar, spotting with the other foot; easier to modulate difficulty on the fly than a freestanding barbell version (shift weight to the spotting leg to lighten it), at the cost of being locked into a fixed movement path.
3. **Leg press / hack squat machine** — lowest barrier to entry; same knee-to-chest, ankle-flexed compression goal, but loses some hip-extension range of motion (most notable on leg press); still gets most of the mobility benefit.

## Ankle-mobility add-on
- Movement: the ATG split squat (credited to Ben Patrick), which directly trains knee-over-toe positioning under load — cited as the main fix for falling backward mid-pistol-squat.
- Cue: keep the heel flat, drive the knee over the big toe.
- Dosage: ~10 reps, 1–2 sets, 2–3x/week (can be done on squat days or rest days); use enough load to make the end-range position genuinely hard.
- Timeline: a few months of consistent work to unlock target mobility, after which regular squat training maintains it without further dedicated work.
