# The true cost of the planche
2026-09-07

[In this video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CcVZFuR-F7I) Woods notes that "40 minutes a week of training has given me an enormous amount" in general strength and physique, but not elite calisthenics skill.

Below is a paraphrased summary, not a transcript.

## Framing
- Splits body-weight-skill ability into two components: "hardware" (raw muscle mass/strength) and "software" (neuromuscular coordination/skill for a specific position).
- Some skills (e.g. handstands) are skill-dominant and can be built mainly through raw strength, per his usual approach. Others — planche, front lever — require both strength *and* coordination to be independently high; raw strength elsewhere (e.g. a big bench press) does not transfer.
- His base 6-movement, full-ROM system builds a lot of general "byproduct" strength (muscle-ups, back levers, short human flags achieved with zero specific practice) but not planche/front-lever-specific strength, because that strength is highly range-specific and doesn't generalize.
- Explicit trade-off noted: training highly specific positions like the planche gives comparatively little benefit outside that position (isolated return), unlike his 6 basic movements.

## The month-long experiment
- Added planche + front lever work on top of his normal program, using his existing 3x/week A/B split (so 3x/week on this too).
- Two session types:
  - **Intense day:** near-max attempts, ~2-second holds of the hardest variation he can manage (straddle front lever, tuck planche), 3 sets each, alternating push/pull, done before his normal full-range bent-arm training.
  - **Volume/lighter day:** scaled-back holds of ~8–12 seconds at near-max effort, using resistance bands for the first time to reduce difficulty, plus dynamic reps (front lever pulls, planche leans) for more range of motion.
- Stated goal targets: advanced tuck / straddle planche held for 3 seconds; a consistent 5-second front lever with clean form.
- Starting point: an "uninspiring" tuck planche and an inconsistent few-second front lever.

## Cost / trade-off
- This add-on roughly doubled his session time (from ~15–20 min to 40+ min).
- Explicitly framed as not recommended for people optimizing for time-efficient training — described as a personal experiment, with a verdict on whether it's "worth it" deferred to a future video.
