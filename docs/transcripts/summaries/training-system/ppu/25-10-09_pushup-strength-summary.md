# How to build pushup strength insanely fast
2025-10-09

[In this video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qoaLWaaWyDM) Woods states the underlying principle: "improving our maximum strength is really simple. We just need to practise it" — at the right difficulty.

Below is a paraphrased summary, not a transcript.

## Core argument — why standard pushups train poorly
- Claims pushup ability (from a first rep to advanced pushup variations) is entirely downstream of horizontal-pushing maximum strength — even endurance gains are attributed to a higher max strength making each rep a smaller fraction of capacity, rather than to endurance training itself.
- Diagnosis of standard pushups: a fixed-difficulty movement spends nearly all working time well below a person's actual strength limit — only the last rep or two (right before failure) approaches genuine maximal effort, so most of the set contributes little to strength/muscle stimulus. For a true beginner, ordinary pushups can be either totally impossible (0% ability) or, once achievable, immediately too easy to keep providing a growth stimulus — with no way to continuously scale difficulty in between.

## The loading variable
- The controlling factor for pushup (and planche) difficulty is the horizontal distance between shoulders and hands: less lean = easier (less demand at the shoulder joint), more forward lean = harder, up to full planche-level difficulty — described as equivalent, in terms of force demand at the shoulder, to loading a heavy barbell bench press despite using only bodyweight.

## Protocol — the self-scaling negative-into-positive method
- Starting position: standard pushup stance, elbows slightly bent.
- **Negative (descent):** rather than lowering under control, progressively increase forward lean until being forced down by the increasing torque demand — continuously pushing the ground away as hard as possible the entire time, varying only the amount of lean (never the effort level, which stays at 100%) to control the rate of descent; continue until chest and hips touch the ground.
- **Positive (ascent):** same principle in reverse — attempt to push back up at max effort; if stationary/stuck, that indicates current strength exactly matches the demand (the target state); as it starts to become too easy or momentum builds, increase the lean again to find a new sticking point; repeat this find-a-sticking-point cycle all the way to full elbow lockout at the top.
- Difficulty-reduction option at any point: bring a knee or foot forward to offload weight from the hands, scalable all the way down to zero load on the hands (verified by being able to remove the hands entirely and stay in position) — meaning the exercise can always be continued regardless of current fatigue level, just at reduced difficulty.

## Dosage and programming
- One continuous set per week for this movement, minimizing rest between reps within the set; continue until wanting a genuine rest, then stop — that marks the week's training for this movement complete.
- Explicitly advises against more frequent sessions or multiple sets per week: growth stimulus is described as saturating quickly, and additional volume mainly adds fatigue/recovery cost without additional strength benefit — recommends using extra training time/energy for the other movement patterns in his 6-movement system instead.
- Primary progress metric: the maximum forward-lean achieved on the first rep of each session (since that's when strength is highest before fatigue accumulates) — tracking this across sessions is the stated way to confirm ongoing progress.
- Time claim: describes this method as requiring under 3 minutes/week of actual working time for this single movement, as part of the broader ~40 min/week, 6-movement system.
