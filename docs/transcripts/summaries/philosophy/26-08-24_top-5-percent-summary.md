# Why I don't train like the pros
2026-08-24

[In this video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=n_sTP-qZWCQ) Woods states plainly: "I'm not that strong. I'm not that flexible. I'm not that muscular."

Below is a paraphrased summary, not a transcript.

## Core argument
- His results (self-estimated top 1–5% of the population for strength/physique/mobility) came entirely from ~40 min/week of strength training plus one solid high-protein meal a day — no additional nutrition tracking or specialized work.
- Analogy used: ~20 hours of focused study puts someone ahead of 19 of 20 people on a topic (top 5%), but reaching genuine world-class expertise takes a decade-plus of dedicated, full-time-level effort — a categorically larger and different investment, not just "more of the same."
- Applied to fitness: his 6-movement, full-range, roughly one-hard-set-per-week system is claimed to capture nearly all the practical strength/mobility/muscle benefits available from training at all.
- Elite/professional-level additions are explicitly named as low-return-on-effort for most people: extra isolation exercises, high training volume (5–7 sessions/week), complex periodization/deloads/exercise cycling, strict calorie and macro tracking, supplements, nutrient timing/carb cycling.

## Sustainability argument
- Any approach needs to be sustained for roughly 5–10+ years to produce genuinely great results, so an approach's long-term adherence matters more than its theoretical ceiling — trying to go all-in on elite-level work risks abandoning training altogether.
- States a person can realistically only pursue "a couple" of true world-class pursuits across a lifetime, given the multi-decade time cost of getting to that level in any one of them.

## Personal example beyond fitness
- Describes applying the same low-time, consistent-effort model to learn functional (not elite/native-level) Spanish over 3 years, done in parallel with training, running a business, and other hobbies (surfing, photography).
