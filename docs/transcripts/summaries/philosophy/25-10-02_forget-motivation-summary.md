# Forget motivation – Do this to reach your goals
2025-10-02

[In this video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A5puXx3UbEA) Woods reframes the whole problem: low motivation matters "if you want to get killer results in fitness and in life" — but not in the way people assume.

Below is a paraphrased summary, not a transcript. This is a mindset/process video applying his training philosophy to motivation broadly, with specific application to exercise, nutrition, and fat loss.

## Core reframe
- Argues persistent lack of motivation for a self-chosen goal isn't a motivation deficit — if it were, the goal wouldn't have been set in the first place — but a signal that the *process* used to pursue it is inefficient (poor return relative to time/effort invested), and the resistance felt is a rational response to that inefficiency.
- Personal example: describes struggling badly with academic writing due to having no real process, contrasted with his current high-volume, low-resistance professional writing — attributes the change entirely to process improvement, not increased motivation or willpower.

## Applied to exercise
- Diagnosis: routines involving many exercises, high weekly hours, and marginal visible results produce a poor results-to-time ratio, which predictably erodes motivation over time — framed as a rational (not weak-willed) response.
- His stated fix: the same 6-movement, ~40-minutes-a-week system referenced throughout his other content, on the reasoning that pushing past roughly 15–20 minutes/movement territory yields steeply diminishing (and eventually negative) returns — describes his own training now as requiring no "hype-up," to the point of feeling eager/restless when travel prevents training.

## Applied to nutrition
- Diagnosis: rigid structured diets (multiple planned meals, food restriction, strict eating windows) carry a real cost to two things people value highly — social life (often built around shared food/drink) and the everyday enjoyment of eating — for comparatively little additional benefit beyond the essentials.
- His stated fix: the same "one intentional high-protein meal a day" approach described in his other nutrition content — meeting protein and nutrient needs in one meal (for him, lunch, chosen to minimize disruption to his social evenings), leaving the rest of the day fully flexible.

## Applied to fat loss
- Diagnosis: a deficit that's too aggressive (i.e. faster than roughly the ~0.5 kg/1 lb-per-week range he generally recommends) produces genuine physiological pushback (hunger, energy crashes) that predictably erodes adherence and often causes rebound weight regain — again framed as a rational signal rather than a discipline failure.
- His stated fix: if a deficit feels genuinely difficult to sustain, slow the rate down (smaller deficit) rather than pushing through via willpower; if progress stalls entirely even at a moderate pace, treat that as a sign of being close to a person's currently sustainable leanness given their muscle mass, and that building more muscle (a surplus phase) — not further restriction — is the more productive next step (echoing the argument in his "why trying to stay lean is killing your gains" video, summarized separately).

## General framing
- Frames "no perfect, resistance-free process exists on day one" — reaching one requires initially working through resistance and using it as diagnostic feedback (what specifically to change or cut) rather than as proof the goal itself, or the person, is the problem.
- Notes that outside guidance/coaching can meaningfully shorten this discovery process by identifying inefficiencies a person can't yet see themselves — offered as a general point, not specific to any one domain.
