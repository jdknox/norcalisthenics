# The 60-second habit that changed my life
2025-12-18

[In this video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wMpkhZdGv9c) Woods states the lesson behind years of self-help reading: "knowledge is useless until it becomes a practise."

Below is a paraphrased summary, not a transcript. This video is about a daily mindset/visualization practice rather than a physical training protocol.

## Core claim
- Identifies a single mechanism common to most effective self-help material he's encountered: clearly deciding what you want and revisiting that vision often enough that day-to-day actions start aligning with it automatically.
- Built a short (roughly 1-minute) daily practice around this mechanism, which he credits as the highest-leverage habit in his routine, crediting it with the trajectory of both his fitness and his business.

## The practice itself
- Sit down, close eyes, and construct a vivid, third-person mental image of the specific life/outcome being aimed for (fitness, business, social, lifestyle goals) — including granular details (location, clothing, activity, company, weather, body language) rather than a vague or general impression.
- Framed as deliberately effortful: the more work required to construct a clear, specific, genuinely exciting image, the more valuable the exercise is considered to be.
- No fixed time target — done until the image feels clear, which may take under a minute or considerably longer depending on the day.

## Three stated reasons this is claimed to work
1. **Forces an actual decision** — a vague notion of "success" isn't a real target; constructing a specific image forces clarifying what is actually wanted, which is treated as a precondition for being able to pursue it directly.
2. **Shifts time horizon** — viewing the whole intended trajectory from a zoomed-out vantage point is described as reducing short-term anxiety/reactivity and improving day-to-day decision-making.
3. **Provides an emotional check** — if the visualized image doesn't feel exciting/motivating, that's treated as useful information that the stated goal may not be the right one, prompting a revision rather than continuing to chase something misaligned.

## Explicit application to fitness
- States that having a clear, specific image of his physical/movement goals is what has kept his own training tightly focused and consistent, and is offered as the reason he's comfortable training only ~40 minutes/week — effort goes disproportionately toward whatever most directly serves the visualized outcome, with everything else deliberately dropped.
- Frames the practice as one component of an individualized personal "system," analogous to his fitness routine — not something to copy exactly, but to adapt as one piece of a broader approach.
