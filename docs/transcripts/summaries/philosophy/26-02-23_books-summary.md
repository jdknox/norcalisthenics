# The books that rewired my brain
2026-02-23

[In this video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JAoaFeyMJBk) Woods traces it back to right after school: "The month that I finished school, I developed an addiction" — to nonfiction reading.

Below is a paraphrased summary, not a transcript. This video is a personal reading list rather than a fitness protocol; content is limited to book titles/authors and his stated one-line takeaway from each (factual references, not reproduced text).

## Filter used for the list
- Only includes books that either changed a core belief he held, or changed something he does daily/regularly — anything short of that is filed as "entertainment," not knowledge worth listing.

## Books and stated takeaways
- **The Moral Animal** (Robert Wright) — reframed emotions as evolved responses to social survival rather than problems to suppress.
- **Anything You Want** (Derek Sivers) — dismantled the idea there's one "correct" way to build a career/business; can be designed around one's own life.
- **Seth Godin** (recommends starting with *Linchpin* or *The Dip*) — reframed his sense of agency/role versus default compliance.
- **The Practice** (Seth Godin) — reinforced consistently showing up and doing the work independent of expecting immediate results.
- **Antifragile** (Nassim Nicholas Taleb, plus his other books generally) — introduced the "barbell" principle (sit at the extremes, not the middle) as a pattern applied across fitness, productivity, and risk generally; credits this as underlying his own asymmetric fitness approach (very hard, brief intensity + very low-intensity recovery, versus a moderate-effort middle ground like endurance training).
- **Think and Grow Rich** (Napoleon Hill) — source of his regular goal-visualization practice (described further in a separate, previously summarized video).
- **The 7 Habits of Highly Effective People** (Stephen Covey) — key takeaway: clarity on the actual desired outcome, and that tasks aren't equally weighted — prioritizing what matters most makes much of the rest irrelevant.
- **How to Win Friends and Influence People** (Dale Carnegie) — described as an easy, high-value read; specifically credits the "avoid criticizing/condemning/complaining" principle for improving his relationships.
- **Deep Work** and **Slow Productivity** (Cal Newport) — shifted his view of productivity from speed to creating space for quality thinking; doing fewer things at a sustainable pace.
- **The Power of Now** and **A New Earth** (Eckhart Tolle) — credited with the largest personal impact on his day-to-day wellbeing; core idea: only the present moment is real, reducing time spent dwelling on past/future.
- **Ignore Everybody** (Hugh MacLeod) — reinforced trusting his own judgment over consensus/other people's opinions when pursuing unconventional work.
- **The War of Art** (Steven Pressfield) — reframed procrastination/resistance as a universal, permanent feature of creative work rather than a personal flaw to eliminate.
- **Stretching Scientifically** (Thomas Kurz) — his stated source for the "build strength at end-range to gain flexibility" approach used throughout his mobility content (see the front-splits and hamstrings summaries).
