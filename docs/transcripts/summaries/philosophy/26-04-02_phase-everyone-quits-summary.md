# The phase where everyone quits
2026-04-02

[In this video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Y8fiLwhOzB0) Woods describes the trap: early on, "you are doing lots of work for not a lot of outcome."

Below is a paraphrased summary, not a transcript. This video is more about mindset/persistence than a specific physical protocol, but includes concrete personal figures.

## Two-phase model of pursuing any goal
1. **The unfocused/exploratory phase** ("fuck around phase"): scattered effort across many directions, with little visible progress, because you don't yet know which specific work actually drives results. This is described as unavoidable — you cannot skip directly to phase 2 without first going through phase 1.
2. **The focused phase**: once you've identified (through trial or guidance) the small subset of work that actually matters, progress becomes fast, obvious, and comparatively easy — and total required time/effort often drops sharply even as results accelerate.
- Central claim: most people quit during phase 1, because extrapolating "lots of effort, little result" indefinitely into the future looks unsustainable — not realizing that the relationship between effort and results changes once the effective work is identified.

## Personal timeline data points given
- **Fitness:** ~3 years of unfocused effort (movement flow, handstands, acrobatics, varied strength exercises, hours of prehab/mobility work weekly) with underwhelming physique/strength results; after narrowing down to focused basic-movement training with adequate nutrition, results accelerated while weekly time investment dropped — eventually reaching the ~40 min/week routine described throughout his other content.
- **Language learning:** contrasts an unfocused first attempt (French, in school) with a more deliberate approach to Spanish (his current project) — high-volume real use (reading and watching content he'd want to consume anyway, real conversations) approximating the target skill directly, rather than generic study methods.
- **Business/content creation:** describes ~2 years in with no real clients/serious business, followed by substantial change 2 years after that — offered as a parallel example of the same two-phase pattern outside fitness.

## Three stated strategies for getting through phase 1
1. **Reframe failure:** the only true failure is quitting — slow or absent visible progress within the first few years is normal, not evidence the goal is unreachable. States a rough rule of thumb that any serious pursuit (a great physique, a language to reasonable fluency, a viable business) takes about 5 years to reach a genuinely satisfying level — roughly 3–4 years to get through the unfocused phase, then a further year or two of clear progress.
2. **Seek guidance over more information:** framed as a shortcut to skip (or shorten) phase 1 — working with someone who already has clarity on what matters lets you redirect effort that would otherwise be spread across many low-value activities into the few that count, rather than continuing to accumulate more information without a way to filter it.
3. **Trust calibrated intuition, tested against real-world results:** treats real-world experimentation and outcomes (not secondhand advice) as the actual mechanism by which someone's judgment becomes reliable over time — repeated real feedback is what lets a person start trusting their own instinct about what will and won't work.
