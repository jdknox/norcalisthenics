# This is why most people never learn anything
2025-10-16

[In this video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OHO95wwamkY) Woods gives his own case study: "I'm currently two years into learning Spanish, which is the fourth foreign language" he's reached conversational fluency in.

Below is a paraphrased summary, not a transcript. This is a language-learning method video, explicitly modeled on the same principles used throughout his fitness content (clear end goal, minimal effective practice, self-directed intensity).

## Stated results
- Under an hour a week invested; describes reading his second Spanish novel, watching Spanish-language TV, and having friendships conducted entirely in Spanish, after roughly 2 years — with the described method producing readable-novel-level ability within the first 6 months.

## Three core principles
### 1. Set a concrete, usage-based goal (not an app/points-based one)
- Rejects app-gamification metrics (streaks, points) in favor of a single functional target: being able to read a novel in the target language. Rationale: once that threshold is crossed, "practice" is no longer needed — ordinary enjoyable reading (already a habit in English) becomes the ongoing exposure mechanism, at no extra time cost.

### 2. Minimal, high-frequency practice — learning happens between sessions, not during them
- Direct parallel to his muscle-growth framing: the actual adaptation (neural "rewiring") happens during rest/consolidation, not during the practice session itself — so short, frequent sessions are prioritized over long cramming sessions.
- States his own regimen for the first 6 months (the ramp-up phase) was just 5 minutes/day.

### 3. Self-directed difficulty, scaled to stay maximally engaging
- Main critique of apps like Duolingo: fixed-difficulty content doesn't scale to the individual, so most practice time ends up well below a person's actual capacity — low engagement, low learning rate (draws a direct analogy to a fixed-weight machine vs. self-scaled bodyweight training).
- His fix: build and control his own practice material rather than using an app.

## His specific daily practice structure (used for the first ~6 months)
1. **Source real examples** — pulled directly from a bilingual example-sentence dictionary (spanishdict.com; wordreference.com cited as a general alternative for other languages), building a personal database of sentence pairs (target language + English meaning), starting with simple, personally relevant phrases and increasing in complexity over time.
2. **Break the sentence down** — analyze why each word/ending/structure is the way it is, but only as deeply as needed to be able to reconstruct the sentence — if a sentence requires knowledge not yet held, either research that specific gap or choose an easier example instead.
3. **Self-test reconstruction** — cover the target-language sentence, read only the English prompt, and attempt to reproduce the original sentence from memory/pattern knowledge — described as the main daily activity, functioning like spaced-repetition flashcards but scaling naturally in complexity as pattern recognition develops.
- States he ended the initial phase with 555 example sentences logged, reaching a point where the practice activity itself became easy/boring — treated as the signal that a harder stage (reading real books) was appropriate.

## Explicit fitness parallel drawn
- Frames the whole approach as applying the same principles as his strength system: a clear defined end goal, the strongest effective stimulus in minimal time, and using subjective engagement/difficulty (rather than a fixed external plan) as the ongoing guide for calibrating intensity.
