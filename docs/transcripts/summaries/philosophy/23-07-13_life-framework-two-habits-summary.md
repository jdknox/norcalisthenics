# Life framework: visualize the goal, minimize the daily practice
Two overlapping blog posts, combined here since the second is essentially the two-habit specific application of the first's general framework:
- "How To Upgrade Yourself – The Two Core Habits" — 2023-08-01 — https://jackhwoods.com/how-to-upgrade-yourself-the-two-core-habits (no video)
- "How To Achieve Anything You Want – My Simple Framework" — 2023-07-13 — https://jackhwoods.com/how-to-achieve-anything-you-want-my-simple-framework (no video)

Below is a paraphrased summary, not a transcript. This general goal-achievement framework overlaps with several later, separately summarized videos (e.g. "how to design a routine," "achieve any goal 10x faster," "forget motivation") — these two posts are among his earliest statements of it.

## The framework ("Achieve Anything You Want")
- Two components at opposite ends of a "clarity spectrum":
  1. **The goal (highest level):** a specific, detailed mental image of the desired end state, built through a *daily* visualization practice (not a one-time goal-setting exercise) — argues most people can't actually picture their goal in specific detail, and that the process of trying to do so daily is itself what clarifies what's genuinely wanted, over time.
  2. **The process (lowest level):** breaking the goal into constituent projects/domains (career, fitness, relationships, etc.), committing to a small measurable weekly practice for each, and tracking completion (his method: a simple spreadsheet with weekly checkboxes).
- Central design principle for the process: make the daily/weekly commitment small enough that it's achievable even on the worst, most time-pressured days — not sized for peak motivation. His own examples at the time: 5 minutes/day of language practice, one set/week for each of 5 strength goals, 5 minutes/day on each of 3 business projects, 5 minutes/day of nonfiction reading.
- States the timeline for any of this to produce visible results is 5–10 years — frames the two-part framework (daily goal visualization + a deliberately minimal, trackable weekly process) as what makes sustaining effort across that timeline realistic.

## The specific two-habit application ("Upgrade Yourself")
- Names strength training and reading nonfiction specifically as the two habits he credits with the majority of his personal growth over ~7 years at time of writing.
- States most people underestimate both how transformative these habits are and how long they take to show results — leading people to start with unsustainably large commitments (e.g. gym 6 days/week, 2 hours of reading/day) that collapse within a few months, rather than a small, indefinitely-sustainable version.
- Stated minimal versions at the time: strength training capped at ~5 minutes per movement × 5 movements = ~25 minutes/week (a 5-movement version of his system, predating the later 6-movement Nordic-curl addition found in his more recent content); reading, 5 minutes on whatever currently holds genuine interest, every weekday (~25 minutes/week) — combined, under an hour/week total for both habits.
- Frames the appeal specifically to people who dislike gyms or reading in the conventional sense: argues that hating wasted time is an asset here, since the minimal-effective-dose framing described above avoids the time-wasting versions of both habits (long unfocused gym sessions, reading books cover-to-cover regardless of interest).
