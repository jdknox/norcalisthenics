# How training for 40 minutes a week took me around the world
2026-07-27

[In this video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PW1R06ICrTc) Woods recalls that "most people who heard about my fitness routine thought it was either crazy" or brilliant.

Below is a paraphrased summary, not a transcript. This video is more reflective/narrative than protocol-heavy, but includes a few concrete claims.

## Origin and framing
- Built the system, and started coaching, after concluding that elite-level time investment wasn't necessary — or even clearly better — for the practical outcomes most people actually want.
- Uses a Pareto-style framing: the minimal ("~20%") effort captures roughly "80%" of the results relevant to general strength, physique, mobility, and health; the remaining "20%" of results (true top-performer/elite level) requires disproportionately more ("80%") effort, and is only worth it for people specifically aiming to compete or specialize.

## Status at time of the video
- 6 years personally using the system; 4+ years coaching it professionally; most recent 6 months spent traveling while teaching remotely.

## Stated next directions (each explored further in separate videos already summarized above)
1. **Pure hypertrophy experimentation** — described as going well in early results, both for himself and clients he's testing it with (see the "multi-set experiment" referenced in his frequency video).
2. **Skill-specific strength work for advanced calisthenics positions** (planche, front lever) — this is the same experiment detailed in "The true cost of the planche."

## Other notes
- Reiterates that the base 6-movement system was originally optimized purely for hypertrophy/general strength, and produced meaningful "byproduct" calisthenics ability (levers, handstand presses, bent-arm planche variants) without any dedicated skill practice.
- Frames his ongoing interest as finding the trade-off point between the simple base system and these two extensions, to be shared as results come in.
