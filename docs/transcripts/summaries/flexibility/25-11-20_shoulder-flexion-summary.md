# Do this to unlock your shoulders (permanently)
2025-11-20

[In this video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZJhQ0V68Zew) Woods opens with the stakes: "If you can't easily flex your shoulders overhead, you've got problems."

Below is a paraphrased summary, not a transcript. Note: this covers shoulder *flexion* (overhead reaching), the opposite range from his German-hang content, which targets shoulder *extension* (see the separate "best exercise for tight shoulders & chest" summary).

## Core mechanism (same nervous-system model as his other mobility content)
- Uses an analogy: catching a heavy ball overhead with a shoulder too weak to resist further flexion would risk serious joint damage — so the nervous system pre-emptively limits overhead range via involuntary muscle tension, specifically to prevent being caught in a position too weak to control.
- The fix: build strength in the muscles that extend the shoulder (lats, pecs, and others — whichever are the actual limiting factor) specifically near the current end range of flexion, which proves to the nervous system that the position is controllable/safe, permanently raising the accessible range.

## Protocol
- Setup: hands placed on a sturdy wall, rings, or a hip-height bar at roughly hip height, arms straight.
- Movement: lean/move toward increasing the angle between torso and upper arm (cue: bring chest toward the ground) until reaching near end range — enough to feel involuntary tension in the stretched muscles, not necessarily full end range.
- Contraction: at that point, actively try to pull the hands back down toward the ground (cued as "closing a car boot" or "pulling down a garage door") with straight arms, flexing the stretched muscles as hard as possible for a few seconds, then relaxing out of the position — that's one rep.
- Expect small increases in accessible range rep to rep within a session as the nervous system "relaxes" into the tested range; this is normal and not itself the mechanism of lasting change (which comes from the underlying strength gain built over sessions).

## Dosage
- One set is sufficient; additional sets optional if still feeling productive, with diminishing returns.
- Frequency: roughly twice a week, placed after upper-body strength training (pairs naturally with an upper-body-twice-a-week split).
- Framed as a finite project: work toward a target (see below), then stop — ongoing maintenance isn't required once strength training itself continues.

## Goal / target movement
- Suggested target: a solid back bridge (hands and feet on the ground, chest to the sky, arms and legs extended) as a practical test of overhead shoulder range.
- Once achieved to a satisfying level, states this range is retained via ordinary full-range strength training without further dedicated work — recommends pairing this (shoulder flexion) with German hangs (shoulder extension, covered separately) for full upper-body mobility coverage.
