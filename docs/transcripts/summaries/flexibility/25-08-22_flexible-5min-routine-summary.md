# How to get flexible forever in a 5 minute session
2025-08-22

[In this video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P8tYVm0PeNE) Woods opens with a caveat: "Most people don't need that much flexibility, but not having enough" to move freely is a real problem worth fixing.

Below is a paraphrased summary, not a transcript. This is the guided full-body-lower routine demonstration; the underlying mechanism (nervous-system end-range strength model) is covered in more depth in his separately summarized flexibility-theory videos.

## Overview
- Presents a single combined ~5-minute session covering five lower-body flexibility movements, each trained using the same principle: pull/load into the current end range, then actively flex the stretched muscles as hard as possible (not passive holding), for reps rather than static holds.
- Frequency: once or twice a week while building range (twice recommended for faster progress); once satisfied with accessible range, states it can be dropped to only occasional use — explicitly not treated as an indefinite maintenance practice.
- General rep target used across movements: roughly 10 reps per side/movement as a practical benchmark, though the actual stopping cue is diminishing returns/reduced usefulness within the set, not a fixed number.

## The five movements
1. **Pike** (toe-touch depth, legs locked/together) — hinge forward pulling with the hands, feel tension in hamstrings/calves, then flex those muscles hard against the pull. Targets lower back and general posterior-chain range (relevant to skills like pike presses).
2. **Pancake** (hip flexion with legs spread) — pull the torso forward using the opposing (hip flexor/core) muscles rather than added weight; flex the inner-thigh muscles hard once tension is felt. If unable to get into position, elevate the hips (sit on something) to reduce the needed angle. Stated personal timeline: roughly 2 months of twice-weekly work to reach chest-to-floor.
3. **Front split** — slide the front foot forward (a sock on a smooth floor works) while keeping the front leg straight and hips squared forward; the actual training focus is the *rear* hip flexor, cued as driving the back knee toward the ground / the front of the rear hip forward, not depth of the front leg.
4. **Side split** — trained via a horse stance (feet parallel, spread to where the thigh is roughly parallel to the ground at the deepest comfortable point), rather than the seated side-split position itself, to reduce knee stress while building the same hip-abduction strength; widen the stance over time as strength allows. (Method credited to the book *Stretching Scientifically*, also referenced in his separately summarized reading-list video.)
5. **Pigeon** (external hip rotation / glute stretch) — lower leg roughly perpendicular to the torso, knee near 90°; elevate the knee with a cushion if needed early on; cue is driving the front ankle into the ground, resisting deeper movement, rather than passively sinking into the position.

## Practical note
- States the full five-movement routine (excluding any test reps) took him about 5 minutes 40 seconds to complete, done back-to-back with no rest required between movements.
