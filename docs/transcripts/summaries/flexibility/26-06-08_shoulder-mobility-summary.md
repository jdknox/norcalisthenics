# Stop stretching your shoulders. Do this instead.
2026-06-08

[In this video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EvwHnRd9HCk) Woods reframes the issue: "the problem is not that your shoulders are too tight" — it's that they aren't strong enough in end range.

Below is a paraphrased summary, not a transcript.

## Core principle
- Same nervous-system-threshold model as his other mobility content: shoulders feel tight because they aren't strong in the deep end-range positions, not because the tissue itself is too short — so build strength there instead of stretching.
- Framed as a finite project (weeks to a few months), not an ongoing daily practice — once the target range is reached, it's maintained without further dedicated work.

## Movement 1 — Cat stretch (overhead flexion)
- Setup: hands anchored between hip and shoulder height (rings, bar, or wall).
- Cue: actively pull out of the overhead position (like a ski-erg pull) — flex whichever muscles (pecs, lats, shoulder muscles) naturally tense up, pulling as hard as possible rather than trying to relax into the stretch.
- As muscles fatigue/release within the set, ease deeper into the range and repeat the contraction cycle.

## Movement 2 — German hang (shoulder extension)
- Setup: supinated (palms-facing-you) grip on rings or a bar; enter via a skin-the-cat rotation, or by setting the rings lower and jumping to an inverted position first.
- Cue: same principle — flex the muscles that tense (mainly chest and front of shoulder) as if pulling yourself back out of the position, rather than relaxing into it.
- Important form note: come out of the hold by actively pulling back out, not by dropping — this is what builds the strength driving the mobility gain.

## Protocol
- Alternate between the two movements for 2–5 sets total; more than one set is described as clearly beneficial (progressively deeper range within a session), with diminishing returns after about 5.
- Stop when it starts to feel unproductive, becomes a drag, or muscles start to feel off.
- Frequency: after upper-body strength training (not before — doing it beforehand negatively affects strength output), ideally twice a week alongside upper-body training days; can be done every other day if desired.
- Either hold for time or do it as ~10 reps moving in and out of the position — both are treated as equally valid.
