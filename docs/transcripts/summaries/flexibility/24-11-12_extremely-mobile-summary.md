# How to get extremely mobile: A complete guide
2024-11-12

[In this video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I6OKvMyN2uc) Woods states the goal plainly: "I want to share how you can get very mobile, very fast," without stretching routines.

Below is a paraphrased summary, not a transcript. The "mobility is just strength trained through full range of motion" argument overlaps with several other summarized mobility videos — this one's distinct contribution is framing mobility loss as a default trajectory of untrained adulthood, and a clean two-step "checklist" for getting it via strength training alone.

## Core reframe
- Rejects the idea that stiffness, soreness, and declining range of motion are a normal, inevitable part of aging — argues these are the predictable result of a training pattern (or lack of one) compounding over years, not age itself; cites working with clients in their 50s–60s who've reversed these issues as evidence, contrasted with less-mobile people decades younger.
- Central claim: mobility *is* strength — specifically, strength trained through the full range of motion of a joint. Strength gained only in a partial range (as with most fixed-resistance training) doesn't transfer to the untrained end ranges, which is exactly where people typically feel tight, stiff, or injury-prone.

## Two-step checklist (the specific structure of this video)
1. **Make sure training is actually effective** — i.e., producing real weekly strength gains and monthly visible physique change; if not, the issue lies there before mobility can be addressed at all.
2. **Train that effective strength work through the full range of motion**, not just the strongest portion of it — flagged as the step most people miss, since the weakest end-range portions require reduced load to train safely, which fixed-resistance training makes awkward (it would require either adding extra light work or reducing intensity for the whole exercise).
- States that self-scaled bodyweight training solves this naturally: intensity can be kept maximal through the strong-mid range and then reduced as needed once approaching the joint's genuine end range, so every rep can cover full range of motion at each point's own appropriate difficulty — no separate mobility session required.

## Scope — when extra work IS warranted
- For general/functional mobility (getting up and down freely, sport, injury resistance, feeling limber), this two-step approach is described as sufficient on its own with zero added time.
- For specific extreme-range goals beyond general function (splits, back bridges, pancake, pike — relevant mainly to dancers, gymnasts, acrobats), states the same maximum-intensity technique can be applied directly to those extreme ranges, but frames this as an advanced add-on only relevant to a small minority already well past the basics — not a general recommendation.
