# How to unlock the front splits permanently
2026-09-14

[In this video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WirgvuGxg0c) Woods explains that "you unlock a position like this once" — the point of the video.

Below is a paraphrased summary, not a transcript.

## Core mechanism
- Flexibility limits are framed as a nervous-system safety threshold, not a fixed property of the muscle — the same "learn once, keep mostly forever" pattern as strength/muscle memory.
- The fix is to strength-train the muscles that resist the stretch (contract them hard at end range) rather than passively stretch — this teaches the nervous system the position is safe, and the threshold shifts outward on its own.
- Target muscles for the front splits specifically: hip flexors of the rear leg, hamstrings of the front leg.

## Protocol
- Movement: reps of sliding in and out of the deepest comfortable range (not holding a static stretch, not forcing a full split).
- Setup: hips kept facing forward; back foot on a slick surface or slider so it can glide; back knee padded (it drives into the ground); hands used on an elevated platform to offload bodyweight as needed — use only as much hand assistance as necessary to reach the deepest comfortable range.
- Cue on the way out of the stretch: actively flex/scissor the stretched muscles — back leg drives down and forward (knee toward the ground), front leg pulls down and back with the knee locked and heel driving into the ground.
- Form keys: front knee locked straight throughout; hips kept square (driving the back hip forward, as if placing the front of that thigh flat to the ground).

## Dosage
- ~10 reps per side per set.
- Low rest needed compared to normal strength training — additional sets can be done back-to-back as long as they still feel productive.
- Recommended frequency: 2–3x/week, placed after strength training sessions.
- Expected timeline: fast progress claimed (weeks, not months) once done consistently.

## Framing / scope
- Explicitly secondary to his main 40-min/week strength system — described as useful only if hip/hamstring tightness is a limiting factor for life, training, or sport, not as a goal in itself.
