# The best exercise for fixing tight shoulders & chest
2025-09-11

[In this video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IwWCCVcwUIw) Woods introduces it as "a secret weapon in my training arsenal that I barely use nowadays" — but credits it with saving his training early on.

Below is a paraphrased summary, not a transcript. This covers shoulder *extension* specifically (opposite range from the separate "unlock your shoulders permanently" summary, which covers shoulder flexion) — this is the fuller standalone treatment of the German hang, versus its brief mention alongside other topics elsewhere in his content.

## Why this specific issue occurs
- Diagnosis: building substantial horizontal-pushing strength (chest/anterior shoulder) without building matching range of motion in the same muscles can lead to tightness, hunched posture, and — in more severe cases — sternum-area pain that can stop pushing training altogether (his own experience, and cited as common among heavy bench pressers and some of his students; a fuller account of his own sternum injury is in the previously summarized training-injuries video).
- Reasoning: most functional mobility is covered by ordinary full-range strength training, but pushing-dominant strength gain specifically outpaces the matching extension range unless it's deliberately addressed.

## Core mechanism (same nervous-system model as his other mobility content)
- Targets shoulder extension range specifically (the range used to bring the arm down/back from overhead) — building strength near the current end range of this motion is what permanently increases accessible range, rather than passive stretching.

## The movement — German hang
- Setup: rings or an overhead bar, supinated grip (palms facing you, as if drinking soup) — chosen partly because it also strengthens/preps the elbows for future ring work.
- Entry: jump directly into an inverted position (hips above shoulders) rather than pulling up into it via a tucked front-lever-style movement, which would add an unrelated strength requirement.
- Once inverted, extend the legs out and perform reps of lowering as deep as currently controllable while extending down toward the ground, at each rep actively flexing the stretched chest/shoulder muscles as if pulling back out of the position — the entire descent is treated as active strength work, not passive stretching, and any rep can be aborted by dropping the feet to the ground if control is lost.
- End-goal standard: a full-depth German hang, held stably with neither feet nor knees touching the ground.

## Dosage
- Target: roughly 10 reps per session as a baseline, regardless of how deep any individual rep gets.
- Frequency: once or twice a week, placed after upper-body strength training (so the relevant muscles are already warmed up and strength work isn't compromised).
- Framed as a finite project: worked on consistently until the full-depth standard is reached (his own timeline: unlocked roughly 7–8 years prior to filming, and rarely needed since), then dropped — maintained automatically via ongoing strength training, revisited only occasionally (his stated cadence: about once every couple of months) if feeling stiff or experiencing recurring tightness/pain.
