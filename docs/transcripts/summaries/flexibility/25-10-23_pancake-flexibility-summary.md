# Stop stretching. Do this instead.
2025-10-23

[In this video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uiKW2lji5uU) Woods sets the priority: "I got a lot of things I want to do with my time" and stretching isn't one of them.

Below is a paraphrased summary, not a transcript. This video focuses specifically on the pancake stretch (hip flexion with straight, spread legs) as his top-priority flexibility goal.

## Why the pancake specifically
- Stated as his single highest-priority flexibility target if only one could be chosen: building it to a decent level is claimed to unlock straddle-leaning skills, and — combined with sufficient overhead pushing strength — the full spectrum of handstand-related entries (straddle press through stalder press), plus general hip-opening benefits.

## Core mechanism (same nervous-system model as his other mobility content)
- Demonstrated via a simple test: sitting with legs straight and spread, hinging forward — most people hit an involuntary point of muscular tension well before any physical joint limit, which is the nervous system pre-emptively restricting range it doesn't yet trust as safe/controllable.
- Fix: build strength specifically near that current end-range point (rather than passively stretching past it), which teaches the nervous system the position is controllable, permanently expanding the accessible range.

## Protocol
- Beginner version: sit on the front edge of a chair, legs straight and spread (the elevated position gives more leverage than the floor). Intermediate/advanced: same movement seated directly on the floor.
- Movement: hinge at the hips, working toward bringing the belly button toward the ground, using an anchor (chair edge, feet, or an object in front) for leverage as needed.
- At the point where involuntary tension is felt in the stretched inner-leg/hip muscles (not necessarily full end range), actively flex those muscles as hard as possible for a few seconds (creating opposing tension — pulling in vs. resisting outward), then relax out of the position — that's one rep.
- Expect the accessible range to increase noticeably rep to rep within a session; this is described as normal (temporary comfort/confidence gain) prior to any actual strength adaptation, which accrues over multiple sessions.

## Dosage
- One set is enough to get most of the benefit; additional sets optional (diminishing returns), can be done for as many reps as still feel productive/enjoyable.
- Frequency: once or twice a week (twice recommended if trying to reach the goal faster).
- Timing: on strength-training days, do it after training (preserves max strength for the actual lifts, and the body is already warmed up for better range); can also be done on off days.
- Framed as a finite project (a few months to reach chest-to-ground or close to it), after which it's dropped entirely rather than maintained as ongoing practice — kept "in the back pocket" for occasional use if feeling stiff.

## Overall framing
- Explicitly secondary to his main strength training: states that once full-range strength training in the six basic movement patterns is dialed in, most people won't feel they need specific flexibility work like this at all — this is offered as an optional add-on for a person who specifically wants the pancake/related skills.
