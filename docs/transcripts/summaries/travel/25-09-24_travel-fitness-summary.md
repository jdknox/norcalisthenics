# How to actually keep fit while travelling
2025-09-24

[In this video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZNGXggihLaQ) Woods frames the usual dilemma: travel normally forces a choice between letting progress "kill all the momentum you had, watch the progress you're making, slip away" — or a third option.

Below is a paraphrased summary, not a transcript.

## Context / when to bother maintaining a routine while traveling
- For trips under ~1 month (or under ~2 months total travel across a year), argues maintenance isn't really necessary at all — the gap is small relative to a full training year and functions more like a planned deload; suggests treating a short trip as a low-stakes test of whether a person's usual routine is actually driving their results (if a short break changes nothing, that's informative either way).
- For longer trips, the strategies below become more relevant; states that learning to pack up a working routine and run it anywhere is itself valuable, since it demonstrates how minimal the actual requirements are.

## Training on the road — two tiers
### Zero-cost / zero-prep version
- Outdoor public workout parks/calisthenics gyms (found via mapping apps) are reported as available in most major cities/countries — chin-up bars for vertical pull, parallel bars for vertical push, horizontal push, and rows (with feet on ground or grounded for assistance).
- Legs: single-leg squats and Nordic curls done with a training partner spotting/anchoring, using the partner's bodyweight and the floor in place of equipment.

### Preferred ("premium") version
- Gymnastics rings (his example: 28mm wooden rings, ~1.4 kg) packed and slung over a tree branch or park structure — covers all four upper-body movements identically to home training.
- Legs: a local gym day-pass (cited range: $10–40/week depending on country) used specifically for loaded single-leg squats (two dumbbells as a substitute for a weighted vest — his example: two 30 kg dumbbells replacing a 60 kg vest, spotting with the other leg) and Nordic curls (using a Smith machine or a barbell/squat rack loaded heavy, with a bench underneath, for a secure setup).
- Split: kept identical to his normal 3-session/week structure (push+pull one day, legs another, push+pull again), but explicitly flexible — e.g., consolidating into a single gym-pass day if that's more convenient for a given trip.
- Cardio: states walking while exploring a new place inherently covers this component with no separate effort needed.

## Nutrition on the road
- Same core principle as his standard approach: hit a protein target (his example: ~100 g) early in the day, which then allows relatively unrestricted eating/drinking for the rest of the day (local food, snacks, alcohol) without disrupting body composition, because appetite is already satisfied and regulated.
- Tiered options for hitting the protein target while traveling, by convenience:
  - Minimal effort: ready-made high-protein drinks/yogurts (his example: a ~2andmeasrode product at ~25 g protein each, roughly €2, found in a European supermarket).
  - Moderate effort: a self-assembled "picnic" of processed meats, cheeses, and high-protein yogurts.
  - Most nutritious: cooking or eating whole animal protein directly (steak, burgers, local barbecue) where accessible and affordable.
  - Contingency: a fast-food option (his example: a McDonald's Quarter Pounder eaten mainly for the meat) used once during a full day of airport transit as a practical fallback.
- Claims this approach is typically cost-neutral or cheaper overall, since the protein target is met cheaply early in the day, reducing the urge to over-order at restaurant meals later.

## Fat-loss strategy specifically
- Explicit recommendation: do not attempt an intentional calorie deficit while traveling — references his standard annual structure (no more than ~12 weeks/year in a deficit, the rest spent at maintenance or in a muscle-gain surplus) and argues travel windows are better used as part of that non-deficit majority of the year.
- If already mid-deficit before a trip and feeling apprehensive about pausing it: frames this as a useful signal to take an actual break (at least a couple of weeks) to reset hormonally/behaviorally, rather than trying to sustain restriction through travel — and explicitly discourages trying to debug an underperforming fat-loss process while traveling, since too many extra variables are in play; save troubleshooting for the normal home routine.
