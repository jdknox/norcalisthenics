# Woods Levels — 7-level strength progression

Source: full transcript of *"How to turn fitness into an addictive video game"*
(jackhwoods.com, 2025-06-24).

Covers the four upper-body movement patterns. The two lower-body patterns
(single-leg squat, Nordic curl) use the same seven-level scheme and are
listed briefly at the end.

Transcription note: the published transcript is auto-generated and error-heavy
("plange"/"plan" = planche, "front labour" = front lever, "hand stamp" =
handstand, "single X squat" = single-leg squat). Corrected silently below.
The one place where an error changes the meaning is flagged with ⚠️.

---

## Planche pushup — horizontal push

| L | Criterion |
|---|---|
| 1 | Knees down, all weight off the hands, full ROM |
| 2 | Up on feet, add forward lean — increasing horizontal distance between shoulders and hands |
| 3 | Tuck planche pushup. Hips need not be level; tuck tight as a ball, knees to chest, any ROM |
| 4 | Hips level with shoulders for some ROM, before they drop |
| 5 | From hips-level, extend out at the hip while keeping hips level |
| 6 | Advanced tuck — 90° at the hip, some ROM |
| 7 | Full lay for some ROM. Easiest at the bottom → bent-arm planche |

**Not in his system:** elevating the feet. Level 2 is forward lean with feet
on the floor; level 3 goes straight to feet-off-the-ground tuck planche.
Feet elevation appears only in the handstand pushup progression.

---

## Front lever row — horizontal pull

| L | Criterion |
|---|---|
| 1 | Walk back, all weight on the feet |
| 2 | Let the body come under the rings, push the rings away toward the feet — increasing horizontal shoulder-to-hand distance |
| 3 | Tuck front lever row, any ROM |
| 4 | Hips level with shoulders for some ROM, before you have to pull them up |
| 5 | Extend out keeping hips level; tuck back to a ball as soon as that's impossible and keep training |
| 6 | Advanced tuck — 90° at the hip |
| 7 | Full lay — easiest at the bottom, i.e. a full front lever |

---

## Handstand pushup — vertical push

| L | Criterion |
|---|---|
| 1 | Standing, working through the motion, no weight on the hands |
| 2 | Feet back into a pike position, starting to load weight under the hands |
| 3 | Elevate the feet to stack more weight on the hands |
| 4 | Legs on the straps — shoulder-stand position at the bottom, flick up into it |
| 5 | From that shoulder-stand position, start to push up — any ROM with straps assisting |
| 6 | Some unassisted range: either the negative (pushed to the top with strap assistance) or an unassisted positive |
| 7 | Move side to side, loading more weight onto one arm — especially on the negatives |

**Safety note (his, verbatim in intent):** at level 4, spread your legs when
flicking up into the shoulder-stand, or be ready to crash. He says everyone
makes this mistake.

## One-arm chin-up — vertical pull

These are **test reps performed before the training set**, not the training
itself. See "Measurement" below.

| L | Test |
|---|---|
| 1 | Two-arm dead hang |
| 2 | Free single-arm dead hang |
| 3 | Two-arm chin-up, partial ROM — any part of the range at full bodyweight |
| 4 | Full ROM chin-up, dead hang to wrists touching chest ⚠️ |
| 5 | Partial-ROM one-arm negative — whatever range you're strongest on |
| 6 | Full-ROM one-arm negative, wrist-touching-chest to dead hang, under control |
| 7 | Positive one-arm chin-up for at least partial ROM |

⚠️ **Level 4 ambiguity.** The transcript reads "full range of motion one arm
chin up." That breaks the ordering — level 5 is a *partial* one-arm negative
and level 7 is a *partial* one-arm positive, both of which are harder than a
full one-arm positive would be. Almost certainly a full **two-arm** chin-up.
Confirm against the video if it matters.

## Measurement rules

**Bilateral movements** (planche pushup, front lever row, HSPU): measured by
the highest progression hit *during training*.

**Unilateral movements** (one-arm chin-up, single-leg squat): measured by a
*test rep before the set*. Training at these looks essentially the same at
every level — always max intensity, full ROM, scaling assistance.

**Partial ROM on the first rep is the entire criterion.** You only have to hit
the progression for some range of motion on your first rep. Explicitly:
completing an entire rep at a progression indicates it is *way too easy* —
that's not what he wants to see.

**Scale down freely afterward.** Once the level is touched on rep one, drop
back through lower progressions to keep getting full-ROM reps at max
intensity for the rest of the set.

## Bonus unlocks

| Level | Unlocks |
|---|---|
| 4 | Muscle-up — dip strength from planche pushups, transition from front lever rows, pull from chin-ups. Needs level 4 on all three |
| 5 | Handstand balance — with practice on kick-ups and alignment |
| 6 | Press-to-handstand (straddle → pike → full), back lever |

## Lower body (for completeness)

**Single-leg squat** — test rep before the set:
L1 bodyweight two-leg squat · L2 negative single-leg squat · L3 full-range
unassisted single-leg positive · L4 +10 kg · L5 +20 kg · L6 +30 kg · L7 +40 kg.

**Nordic curl** — no level 1–3 criteria given; he says keep working on it and
picks it up at level 4:
L4 some unassisted positive ROM, hips may bend, anything before the knee
reaches 90° (femur perpendicular to ground) counts · L5 full negative without
hands, chest touching down · L6 no hands on the positive, lift chest off the
ground · L7 add weight to slow it down.

---

## How to use this

Find your current level for each pattern. Your immediate target is the next
level, not the end goal. Keep current levels written down and record new ones
as they're unlocked — this is his own stated tracking recommendation, and it
is the only tracking the system requires.
